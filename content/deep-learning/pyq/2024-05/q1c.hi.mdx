**In simple words:** dropout ka matlab hai ki training ke dauraan hum har step mein kuch neurons ko random tarike se band (off) kar dete hain, taaki network kisi ek neuron par zyada depend na kare. <Cite id="srivastava2014" />

**Definition.** Dropout ek regularization method hai jo har training step mein randomly chune hue neurons ko zero kar deta hai. Bade networks overfit karte hain kyunki neurons ek-doosre par depend karne lagte hain aur milkar training data ko ratta maar lete hain; dropout ye dependence todta hai.

**Ye kaise kaam karta hai.**

1. Keep probability $p$ chuno, jaise hidden layers ke liye 0.5.
2. Har training batch mein har neuron ko probability $p$ ke saath rakho, warna off karo; agle batch mein ek alag random set drop hota hai.
3. Test time par saare neurons rakho. Ya to weights ko $p$ se scale karo, ya *inverted dropout* use karo, jisme training ke waqt kept values ko $1/p$ se scale up karte hain.

**Worked example (inverted dropout).** Given: chaar hidden neurons ka output 2, 4, 6, 8 hai; $p = 0.5$; neuron 2 aur 4 off hain. To find: aage bheji gayi values.

1. Off neurons ka output 0 ho jaata hai.
2. Kept values ko $1/p = 2$ se scale karo: $2 \times 2 = 4$ aur $6 \times 2 = 12$.

**Answer: layer aage 4, 0, 12, 0 bhejti hai.**

<Box kind="verify">

Bahut saare batches ka average lo. Neuron 0.5 probability se rakha jaata hai aur tab apni value ka do guna deta hai, to neuron 1 ka expected output $0.5 \times 4 + 0.5 \times 0 = 2$ hai, yani original value. Neuron 3 ke liye $0.5 \times 12 = 6$, ye bhi original. Matlab dropout average mein layer ka output nahi badalta.

</Box>

**Advantages:** loss badle bina overfitting kam karta hai; bahut sasta (bas ek random 0/1 mask); bahut saare thinned networks ke average jaisa kaam karta hai; har neuron ko khud useful features seekhne par majboor karta hai. **Limitation:** signal noisy hone se training slow hoti hai, aur $p$ tune karna padta hai. <Cite id="gfg-dropout" />

Exam mein English mein likho: "Dropout randomly switches off neurons during training to reduce overfitting."
