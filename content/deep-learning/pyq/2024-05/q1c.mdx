**In simple words:** during training, dropout switches off some neurons at random, so no neuron can depend on its neighbours and the network cannot memorize the training data. <Cite id="class-dl-chp2" loc="slide 107" />

**Definition:** dropout is a regularization technique that randomly turns OFF (drops) some neurons during training. The dropped neurons do not take part in forward propagation, backpropagation or weight updates. Each training iteration uses a slightly different network. <Cite id="class-dl-chp2" loc="slide 107" />

**Why it is needed:** in deep networks some neurons become too dependent on others. If one neuron learns a wrong pattern, many others learn it too, and the network memorizes the training data (overfitting). <Cite id="class-dl-chp2" loc="slide 107" />

<Img id="class-regularization-dropout-network" caption="The same network without dropout and with dropout during training (crossed neurons are dropped)." />

**How it solves overfitting:** random drop → less dependency → learn general patterns → overfitting reduces. Every neuron has to learn useful features on its own, because any neuron may be dropped at any time. The textbook sees it as an ensemble of many thinned networks that share weights. <Cite id="class-dl-m2" loc="p. 38" />

**Dropout rate (p):** the percentage of neurons turned OFF; $p = 0.5$ is the most common for hidden layers. **Training versus testing:** random neurons are dropped in training; in testing all neurons are active. <Cite id="class-dl-chp2" loc="slide 108" />

**Advantages:** reduces overfitting, improves generalization, prevents neuron dependency, makes the network more robust, simple and widely used. **Disadvantage:** training is slightly slower, a very high rate may cause underfitting. <Cite id="class-dl-chp2" loc="slide 108" />

**Conclusion:** dropout makes the network less dependent on any single neuron, so it generalizes better to unseen data.
