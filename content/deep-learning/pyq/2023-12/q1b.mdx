**In simple words:** we add up the squared errors of the model $y = xw$ over all $N$ pairs, find the slope of that total error with respect to $w$, and move $w$ a small step against the slope. <Cite id="class-dl-chp2" loc="slide 63" />

```mermaid
%% caption: How w is found step by step with gradient descent.
flowchart LR
  A["Start with w"] --> B["Predict y = x w"]
  B --> C["Error E (sum of squares)"]
  C --> D["Gradient dE/dw"]
  D --> E["w = w - alpha dE/dw"]
  E -->|"repeat"| B
```

<Sum>

<Given>

$N$ pairs $(x_n, y_n)$, prediction $x_n w$, learning rate $\alpha$.

</Given>

<Find>

$E$, $\partial E / \partial w$ and the update rule.

</Find>

<Steps>

1. **Error of one pair:** $y_n - x_n w$. Square it so that positive and negative errors cannot cancel and add over all pairs. The $\tfrac{1}{2}$ is a convention that cancels the 2 when we differentiate: $E = \dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N} (y_n - x_n w)^2$.
2. **Differentiate (chain rule):** the square gives $2(y_n - x_n w)$ and the inner part gives $-x_n$, so $\dfrac{\partial E}{\partial w} = -\sum_{n=1}^{N} (y_n - x_n w)\, x_n$.
3. **Step against the slope** (the class rule $W_{new} = W_{old} - \eta\, \partial L / \partial W$): $w \leftarrow w - \alpha \dfrac{\partial E}{\partial w} = w + \alpha \sum_{n=1}^{N} (y_n - x_n w)\, x_n$.
4. **Tiny example:** pairs $(1, 2)$ and $(2, 4)$, $w = 0$, $\alpha = 0.1$. Errors 2 and 4, so $E = \tfrac{1}{2}(4 + 16) = 10$. Gradient $= -(2 \times 1 + 4 \times 2) = -10$. New $w = 0 - 0.1 \times (-10) = 1$. New errors 1 and 2, so $E = \tfrac{1}{2}(1 + 4) = 2.5$.

</Steps>

<Answer>

$E = \dfrac{1}{2}\sum (y_n - x_n w)^2$ and $w \leftarrow w + \alpha \sum (y_n - x_n w)\, x_n$. In the example one step moves $w$ from 0 to 1 and $E$ from 10 to 2.5.

</Answer>

<Box kind="verify">

Expand $E$ for the example: $E = \tfrac{1}{2}[(2 - w)^2 + (4 - 2w)^2] = 10 - 10w + 2.5w^2$. Its slope $-10 + 5w$ is $-10$ at $w = 0$ (matches step 4) and zero at $w = 2$, the true value because $y = 2x$. Also $E(1) = 10 - 10 + 2.5 = 2.5$.

</Box>

</Sum>

**Note:** the class files give the update rule $W_{new} = W_{old} - \eta\, \partial L / \partial W$ but not the derivation of $E$; steps 1 and 2 are the standard calculus.

**Conclusion:** $w$ grows when the predictions are too small and shrinks when they are too large, until the error is smallest.
