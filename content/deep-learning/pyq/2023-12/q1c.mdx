**In simple words:** dropout means that while the network is being trained, we randomly switch off some neurons, so the network can never depend too much on any one neuron.

**Definition.** Dropout is a regularization method that temporarily removes randomly chosen neurons (sets their output to zero) at every training step. <Cite id="srivastava2014" />

**Why it is needed.** Large networks overfit: neurons co-adapt, relying on each other's quirks, and together memorize the training data instead of learning general patterns.

**How it works**

1. Choose a keep probability, for example 0.5 for hidden layers.
2. For each training batch, keep every neuron with that probability and switch the rest off; the next batch drops a different random set.
3. At test time keep all neurons and scale the weights by the keep probability, so the average signal matches training (inverted dropout scales up during training instead). <Cite id="d2l-dropout" />

**How it reduces overfitting**

- No neuron can rely on particular neighbours, so each must learn features that are useful on their own.
- Every step trains a different thinned network; the final network acts like their average (an ensemble), which cancels quirks of the training data. <Cite id="gfdl-ch7" loc="§7.12" />

**Advantages:** cheap, works with most architectures. **Disadvantage:** training takes longer to converge. <Cite id="gfg-dropout" />

**Conclusion:** dropout is a cheap, widely used way to generalize better.
