**In simple words:** overfitting is when a network memorizes its training data instead of learning the pattern; underfitting is when it has not learned the pattern at all. Think of a student who memorizes one paper (overfitting) and one who has barely studied (underfitting).

**Overfitting.** The network fits training data, noise included, so closely that it does badly on new data. Training error is low, validation error is high (high variance).

**Underfitting.** The network is too simple, or trained too little, to capture the pattern. Both errors are high (high bias). <Cite id="gfdl-ch5" loc="§5.2" />

**How to detect it.** Plot both errors against epochs. If training error keeps falling while validation error rises, the network is overfitting. If both stay high, it is underfitting.

| | Underfitting | Good fit | Overfitting |
|---|---|---|---|
| Training error | High | Low | Very low |
| Validation error | High | Low | High |
| Fix | Bigger model, longer training | None | More data, dropout, early stopping |

**Example:** a spam filter that flags every email from one sender seen in training is overfitting. <Cite id="gfg-overfitting" />

**Conclusion:** the goal is a balance where the network does well on both training and new data. Regularization is the main tool for that balance. <Cite id="gfdl-ch7" />
