**In simple words:** underfitting means the model learns too little; overfitting means it learns too much (it memorizes), so both fail on new data. <Cite id="class-dl-chp2" loc="slide 93" />

**Underfitting:** the model is too simple to learn the important patterns, so it performs poorly on both training and testing data (high bias). **Overfitting:** the model learns the training data too well, including noise and unnecessary details, so it performs very well on training data but poorly on testing data (high variance). **Good fit:** it learns the correct patterns and performs well on both. <Cite id="class-dl-chp2" loc="slides 93 and 94" />

<Img id="class-regularization-fit-curves" caption="Underfit (too simple), good fit (just right) and overfit (fits the noise also)." />

**Fruit example:** underfitting calls an apple a banana. Overfitting learns "an apple always has a black spot", so a new apple without the spot is "Not Apple". <Cite id="class-dl-chp2" loc="slide 93" />

| | Underfitting | Good fit | Overfitting |
|---|---|---|---|
| Model complexity | Too simple | Balanced | Too complex |
| Training accuracy | Low | High | Very high |
| Testing accuracy | Low | High | Low |
| Generalization | Poor | Excellent | Poor |

**How to identify:** underfitting has high training and validation loss; overfitting has falling training loss but rising validation loss. <Cite id="class-dl-chp2" loc="slide 95" /> **Causes of overfitting:** complex model, too many epochs, small dataset, noise. **Fixes:** more data, data augmentation, early stopping, dropout, L1/L2, simpler model. **Fixes for underfitting:** more epochs, more complexity, better features. <Cite id="class-dl-chp2" loc="slide 96" />

**Conclusion:** the aim is a good fit with good generalization.
