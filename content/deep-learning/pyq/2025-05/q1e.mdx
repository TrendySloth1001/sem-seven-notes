**In simple words:** a CNN looks at a picture the way you scan a crowd photo: a small window at a time, asking the same question everywhere. Convolution and pooling find features; flatten and fully connected layers decide the class. <Cite id="class-dl-chp4" loc="slide 4" />

**Definition:** CNN is a deep learning model that automatically learns spatial features from images using convolutional filters. <Cite id="class-dl-chp4" loc="slide 5" />

<Img id="class-cnn-arch-digits" caption="Basic working of a CNN: input, convolution + ReLU, max pooling, flatten, fully connected layer, softmax." />

**Basic working** <Cite id="class-dl-chp4" loc="slide 28" />

1. **Input:** the image is a grid of numbers (RGB = 3 channels).
2. **Convolution + ReLU:** small filters slide over the image (multiply and add) and give feature maps; ReLU turns negatives into 0. Early layers find edges.
3. **Pooling:** reduces height and width, not depth.
4. **Repeat:** deeper layers combine edges into shapes and object parts.
5. **Flatten:** 3D feature maps become a 1D vector.
6. **Fully connected layer + softmax:** gives probabilities; the highest is the predicted class.

**Why it works:** local connectivity, weight sharing (same filter everywhere) and pooling mean far fewer parameters than a fully connected network. <Cite id="class-dl-chp4" loc="slide 5" />

**Conclusion:** CNN = convolution + pooling for features, fully connected layers for the decision.
