**In simple words:** perplexity batata hai ki language model test text se kitna **surprised** hota hai. Jo model asli test sentences ko high probability deta hai wo kam surprised hota hai, yaani uski **perplexity low** hoti hai. Low matlab better. <Cite id="ngram-language-models-slp3-ch3" /> <Cite id="ngram-language-models-wiki-perplexity" />

**Formula.** $N$ words $w_1 \dots w_N$ ke test text ke liye perplexity text ki inverse probability hai, length se scale ki hui:

$$
PP(W) = P(w_1 w_2 \dots w_N)^{-1/N}
$$

Bigram model ke liye ye $PP(W) = \sqrt[N]{\prod_{i} \frac{1}{P(w_i \mid w_{i-1})}}$ ban jaata hai. Power $1/N$ alag lengths ke texts ko comparable banati hai. Ye inverse word probabilities ka geometric mean hai.

**Meaning (weighted branching factor).** Perplexity 4 ka matlab model utna hi unsure hai jaise har step pe next word 4 equally likely choices mein se chunna ho. <Cite id="ngram-language-models-slp3-ch3" />

<Sum>

<Given>

Ek bigram model 3-word test text ko word probabilities $1/2$, $1/4$ aur $1/8$ deta hai.

</Given>

<Find>

perplexity.

</Find>

<Steps>

1. Text ki probability $= \frac12 \times \frac14 \times \frac18 = \frac1{64}$. *Reason: word probabilities ko multiply karo.*
2. Power $-1/N$ lo, $N = 3$: $PP = (1/64)^{-1/3} = 64^{1/3}$. *Reason: formula.*
3. $64^{1/3} = 4$ kyunki $4 \times 4 \times 4 = 64$.

</Steps>

<Answer>

perplexity $= 4$

</Answer>

<Box kind="verify">

Logs use karo: $\log_2 2 + \log_2 4 + \log_2 8 = 1 + 2 + 3 = 6$, 3 se divide karo to 2 bits per word, aur $2^2 = 4$. Same answer.

</Box>

</Sum>

**Yaad rakhne ke points:**

- Same test set pe low perplexity matlab better model (jo model 10,000-word vocabulary ke har word ko equal chance de, uski perplexity 10,000 hoti hai).
- Ise sirf same test data aur vocabulary pe models compare karne ke liye use karo; train aur test sets alag hone chahiye.
- Zero probability se perplexity infinite ho jaati hai, isliye smoothing chahiye.
- Ye **intrinsic** check hai; asli test real task (translation, speech) hai. <Cite id="ngram-language-models-gfg-perplexity" />
