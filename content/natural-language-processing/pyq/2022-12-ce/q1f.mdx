**In simple words:** perplexity tells how **surprised** a language model is by a test text. A model that gives high probability to the real test sentences is less surprised, so it has **low perplexity**. Lower is better. <Cite id="ngram-language-models-slp3-ch3" /> <Cite id="ngram-language-models-wiki-perplexity" />

**Formula.** For a test text of $N$ words $w_1 \dots w_N$, perplexity is the inverse probability of the text, scaled by length:

$$
PP(W) = P(w_1 w_2 \dots w_N)^{-1/N}
$$

For a bigram model this becomes $PP(W) = \sqrt[N]{\prod_{i} \frac{1}{P(w_i \mid w_{i-1})}}$. The power $1/N$ makes texts of different lengths comparable. It is the geometric mean of the inverse word probabilities.

**Meaning (weighted branching factor).** A perplexity of 4 means the model is as unsure as if it had to pick the next word from 4 equally likely choices at every step. <Cite id="ngram-language-models-slp3-ch3" />

<Sum>

<Given>

A bigram model gives a 3-word test text the word probabilities $1/2$, $1/4$ and $1/8$.

</Given>

<Find>

the perplexity.

</Find>

<Steps>

1. Probability of the text $= \frac12 \times \frac14 \times \frac18 = \frac1{64}$. *Reason: multiply the word probabilities.*
2. Take the power $-1/N$ with $N = 3$: $PP = (1/64)^{-1/3} = 64^{1/3}$. *Reason: the formula.*
3. $64^{1/3} = 4$ because $4 \times 4 \times 4 = 64$.

</Steps>

<Answer>

perplexity $= 4$

</Answer>

<Box kind="verify">

Use logs: $\log_2 2 + \log_2 4 + \log_2 8 = 1 + 2 + 3 = 6$, divided by 3 gives 2 bits per word, and $2^2 = 4$. Same answer.

</Box>

</Sum>

**Points to remember:**

- Lower perplexity means a better model on the same test set (a model giving every word of a 10,000-word vocabulary equal chance has perplexity 10,000).
- Use it only to compare models on the same test data and vocabulary; train and test sets must differ.
- A zero probability makes perplexity infinite, so smoothing is needed.
- It is an **intrinsic** check; a real task (translation, speech) is the final test. <Cite id="ngram-language-models-gfg-perplexity" />
