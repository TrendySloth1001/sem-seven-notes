**In simple words:** perplexity tells how surprised a language model is by a text. The lower the perplexity, the better the model.

**Definition:** perplexity is a measurement of uncertainty in the predictions of a language model. It indicates how surprised a model is by the actual outcomes. <Cite id="class-nlp-pyq2" loc="p. 5" />

$$
\text{Perplexity}(P) = 2^{-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log_2 P(w_i \mid w_1, \dots, w_{i-1})}
$$

It is the exponential of the average negative log-likelihood of the words given their context. <Cite id="class-nlp-pyq2" loc="p. 5" />

| Perplexity | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | The ideal score: the model predicts the next word perfectly every time |
| Above 1 | Some uncertainty; the higher, the less confident the model |
| 10 | The model is as uncertain as if it were choosing between 10 different possible next words |

Check of the last row: if every word has probability 1/10, then the perplexity is $(10^{-N})^{-1/N} = 10$.

**Conclusion:** use perplexity to compare language models; the one with the lower value is better.
