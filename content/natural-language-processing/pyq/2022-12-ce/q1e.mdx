**In simple words:** these four names describe how the meanings of different words (or of one word) are related. <Cite id="lexical-semantics-slp3-ch5" />

**1. Homonymy.** Two or more **unrelated** meanings share the same spelling and sound, but they are really different words that happen to look alike. *Bank* (river side) and *bank* (money institution) are homonyms; so are *bat* (animal) and *bat* (cricket). In a dictionary they get separate entries. <Cite id="lexical-semantics-wiki-homonym" />

**2. Polysemy.** One word with several **related** senses. *Mouth* of a person and *mouth* of a river share the idea of an opening; *head* of a body and *head* of a school share the idea of the top or the leader. A dictionary puts them under one entry. <Cite id="ambiguity-challenges-wiki-polysemy" />

**3. Synonymy.** Different words with (nearly) the **same meaning**: *big* and *large*, *couch* and *sofa*, *buy* and *purchase*. Perfect synonyms are rare; the words often differ in style or use (*water* and *H2O*). WordNet groups synonyms into a synset. <Cite id="lexical-semantics-wiki-synonym" /> <Cite id="lexical-semantics-gfg-synonyms" />

**4. Antonymy.** Words with **opposite** meanings. Types: gradable (*hot* and *cold*, with middle values), complementary (*alive* and *dead*, no middle) and relational or reverse (*buy* and *sell*, *up* and *down*). <Cite id="lexical-semantics-wiki-antonym" />

| Relation | Words | Meanings | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Homonymy | Same form | Unrelated | *bank* (river) / *bank* (money) |
| Polysemy | Same word | Related | *mouth* of river / person |
| Synonymy | Different words | Same | *big* / *large* |
| Antonymy | Different words | Opposite | *hot* / *cold* |

**Tip:** if you can see a link between the two senses it is polysemy; if the senses have nothing in common it is homonymy.
