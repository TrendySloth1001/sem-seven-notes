**In simple words:** humans learn language as children and use common sense without effort. A computer has only rules and data, so many things that look easy to us are hard for it. These are the challenges of NLP. <Cite id="ambiguity-challenges-ibm-nlp" />

**Main challenges**

1. **Ambiguity.** One word or sentence can have more than one meaning. This is the biggest challenge. <Cite id="ambiguity-challenges-gfg-ambiguity" />
2. **Context and world knowledge.** The right meaning depends on the situation. "It is cold in here" may really mean "please close the window".
3. **Figurative language.** Idioms, sarcasm and humour break word-by-word reading: "kick the bucket" is not about a bucket.
4. **Variation.** Slang, spelling mistakes, short forms ("gr8"), dialects, and mixed languages such as Hinglish.
5. **Reference.** Pronouns like *he*, *it*, *they* must be linked to the right noun.
6. **Many languages and little data.** Rich morphology and different word order make one method fail on another language, and many languages have few training texts. <Cite id="ambiguity-challenges-slp3" />
7. **Changing language.** New words appear all the time (*selfie*, *doomscroll*), so a fixed dictionary goes out of date.

| Challenge | Example |
|---|---|
| Lexical ambiguity | *bank*: money bank or river bank <Cite id="ambiguity-challenges-wiki-polysemy" /> |
| Syntactic ambiguity | "I saw the man with a telescope": who has the telescope? |
| Idiom | "It is raining cats and dogs" |
| Reference | "Ravi told Amit that he won": who won? |

**Conclusion:** NLP is hard because language is ambiguous, depends on context, keeps changing, and needs world knowledge that computers do not have by default.
