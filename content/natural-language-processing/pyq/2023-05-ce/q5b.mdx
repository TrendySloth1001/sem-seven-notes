**In simple words:** stemming cuts the endings off words so that *connect, connected, connecting, connection* all become *connect*. The **Porter stemmer** (Martin Porter, 1980) does this with a fixed list of suffix-stripping rules, applied in five ordered steps. It is used in search engines so that different forms of a word match. <Cite id="text-preprocessing-porter1980" /> <Cite id="porter-stemmer-gfg-stemming" />

**Basic ideas** <Cite id="porter-stemmer-snowball" />

- Letters are vowels (a, e, i, o, u, and y after a consonant) or consonants. Every word has the form $[C](VC)^m[V]$, and **$m$ is the measure**, the number of vowel-consonant pairs. *tree* $m=0$, *trouble* $m=1$, *troubles* $m=2$.
- A rule is written (condition) SUFFIX1 → SUFFIX2. Conditions: $m>0$, $m>1$; `*v*` stem has a vowel; `*d` stem ends in a double consonant; `*o` stem ends consonant-vowel-consonant where the last is not w, x or y.
- In each step only the longest matching suffix is used.


<Cite id="porter-stemmer-av-stemming" loc="diagram" />

| Step | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1a | SSES→SS, IES→I, SS→SS, S→(nothing) | caresses→caress, ponies→poni, cats→cat |
| 1b | (m>0) EED→EE; (*v*) ED→, ING→ then tidy: AT→ATE, double letter→single, (m=1 and *o)→add E | agreed→agree, plastered→plaster, hopping→hop |
| 1c | (*v*) Y→I | happy→happi |
| 2 | (m>0) ATIONAL→ATE, TIONAL→TION, IZATION→IZE, FULNESS→FUL, OUSNESS→OUS... | relational→relate |
| 3 | (m>0) ICATE→IC, ATIVE→, ALIZE→AL, FUL→, NESS→ | happiness→happi |
| 4 | (m>1) delete AL, ANCE, ER, IC, ABLE, ANT, MENT, ION, ATE, IVE, IZE... | adjustment→adjust |
| 5 | (m>1) E→; (m=1 and not *o) E→; (m>1 and *d and L) drop one L | probate→probat, controll→control |

The examples show the result of that step alone; later steps may change the word further (for example *agree* loses its E in step 5).

<Sum>

<Given>

words *relational*, *hopping*, *happiness*.

</Given>

<Find>

the Porter stem of each.

</Find>

<Steps>

1. *relational*: step 1 no change. Step 2: ATIONAL→ATE since stem *rel* has $m=1>0$: *relate*. Step 3, 4: no change (for ATE in step 4 the stem *rel* needs $m>1$). Step 5: stem *relat* has $m=2>1$, so drop E: *relat*.
2. *hopping*: step 1b: ING is removed because *hopp* has a vowel: *hopp*. It ends in a double consonant (not L, S, Z), so drop one letter: *hop*.
3. *happiness*: step 1a: ends in SS, unchanged. Step 3: NESS→ with stem *happi* ($m=1>0$): *happi*. Nothing else applies.

</Steps>

<Answer>

relational → **relat**, hopping → **hop**, happiness → **happi**

</Answer>

<Box kind="verify">

Recount $m$ for *relat* (r-e-l-a-t = C V C V C): pairs *el*, *at*, so $m=2$, which allows step 5. NLTK's `PorterStemmer` gives the same three stems.

</Box>

</Sum>

**Advantages:** fast, simple, needs no dictionary. **Disadvantages:** over-stemming (*university* and *universe* both become *univers*), under-stemming, non-words (*happi*), and English only. <Cite id="porter-stemmer-av-stemming" />

**Conclusion:** the Porter stemmer strips suffixes in five ordered steps, using the measure $m$ to avoid cutting short words.
