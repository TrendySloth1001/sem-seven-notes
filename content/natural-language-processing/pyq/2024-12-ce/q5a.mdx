**In simple words:** stemming cuts the endings off words so that *connect, connected, connecting, connection* all become *connect*. The Porter stemmer (Martin Porter, 1980) does this with five steps of suffix rules that are applied in order. <Cite id="porter-stemmer-def" /> <Cite id="porter-stemmer-gfg-stemming" />

**Why it is needed:** in search and text classification, different forms of a word should count as one, which also shrinks the vocabulary.

**Basic ideas** <Cite id="porter-stemmer-def" />

- Letters are vowels (a, e, i, o, u, and y after a consonant) or consonants. Any word looks like $[C](VC)^m[V]$, and **$m$ is the measure**: how many vowel-consonant pairs it has. Examples: *tree* $m=0$, *trouble* $m=1$, *troubles* $m=2$.
- Rules look like: (condition) SUFFIX → NEW. Conditions: $m>0$; `*v*` (stem has a vowel); `*d` (ends in a double consonant); `*o` (ends consonant-vowel-consonant, last not w, x, y).
- Only the longest matching suffix in a step is tried.

<Cite id="porter-stemmer-gfg-stemming" loc="diagram" />

| Step | What it does | Rules and examples |
|---|---|---|
| 1a | plurals | SSES→SS (caresses→caress), IES→I (ponies→poni), S→ (cats→cat) |
| 1b | -ed, -ing (if the stem has a vowel) | plastered→plaster, motoring→motor; then fix: hopping→hop (double letter removed), filing→file (e added when m=1 and cvc) |
| 1c | y to i (if stem has a vowel) | happy→happi |
| 2 | long suffixes if $m>0$ | ATIONAL→ATE (relational→relate), IZATION→IZE, FULNESS→FUL |
| 3 | if $m>0$ | ICATE→IC, ATIVE→(nothing), ALIZE→AL, FUL→(nothing), NESS→(nothing) |
| 4 | delete suffixes (AL, ANCE, ER, IC, ABLE, ANT, MENT, ION, ATE, IVE, IZE and others) if $m>1$ | |
| 5 | final tidy | remove E if $m>1$ (or $m=1$ and not `*o`); controll→control |

<Sum>

<Given>

the word *generalizations*.

</Given>

<Find>

its Porter stem.

</Find>

<Steps>

1. **Step 1a:** ends in S (not SS or IES), so remove it: *generalization*.
2. **Steps 1b, 1c:** no -ed, -ing or y ending, so no change.
3. **Step 2:** the suffix IZATION → IZE, stem *general* has $m=3>0$: *generalize*.
4. **Step 3:** ALIZE → AL, stem *gener* has $m=2>0$: *general*.
5. **Step 4:** the suffix AL is deleted since the stem *gener* has $m=2>1$: *gener*.
6. **Step 5:** ends in R, nothing to do.

</Steps>

<Answer>

generalizations → **gener**

</Answer>

<Box kind="verify">

Count $m$ again for *gener* (g-e-n-e-r = C V C V C): the pairs are *en* and *er*, so $m=2$, which is more than 1, so step 4 is allowed. Porter's paper traces the same word to GENER, and NLTK's `PorterStemmer` returns `gener` too.

</Box>

</Sum>

**Advantages:** fast, simple, no dictionary needed, widely used. **Disadvantages:** it can over-stem (*university* and *universe* both become *univers*), under-stem, produce non-words (*happi*, *quickli*), and works for English only. <Cite id="porter-stemmer-wiki-stem" />

**Conclusion:** Porter's stemmer strips suffixes in five ordered steps using the measure $m$ to avoid cutting short words.
