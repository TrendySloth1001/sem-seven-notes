**In simple words:** in a text, the same person or thing is mentioned many times in different ways ("Ravi", "he", "the boy"). Reference resolution is the job of finding which mentions point to the same thing. <Cite id="discourse-reference-slp3-coref" />

**Key terms**

- **Referring expression:** the phrase used to point to something (*he*, *the boy*).
- **Referent:** the real person or thing it points to (Ravi).
- **Anaphora:** a word that points back to an earlier one. In "Ravi lost his pen", *his* points back to Ravi (the **antecedent**). <Cite id="discourse-reference-wiki-coref" />
- **Cataphora:** points forward ("When **he** arrived, Ravi smiled"). <Cite id="discourse-reference-wiki-cataphora" />

**Types of referring expressions:** pronouns (*he, it*), definite noun phrases (*the boy*), demonstratives (*this, that*), proper names, and indefinite phrases (*a boy*, which introduces something new).

**Clues used to resolve a pronoun**

1. Agreement in number, gender and person.
2. Syntax: a pronoun in the same clause usually cannot refer to the subject unless it is reflexive.
3. Recency: nearer candidates are preferred.
4. Subject preference and salience: the topic of the story is likely.
5. Meaning: "The man ate the **apple**; it was sweet" fits *apple*, not *man*.

**Approaches:** rule-based (Hobbs' algorithm, Centering) and machine learning that scores each candidate. <Cite id="anaphora-resolution-ibm-coref" /> <Cite id="anaphora-resolution-tp-nlp" />

**Uses:** summarization, question answering, translation, information extraction.

**Conclusion:** reference resolution links every mention to its referent using agreement, syntax, recency and meaning.
