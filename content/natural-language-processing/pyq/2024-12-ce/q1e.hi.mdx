**In simple words:** ambiguity ka matlab hai ki text ko ek se zyada tareeke se samjha ja sakta hai. **Lexical ambiguity** mein confusion ek word se aata hai. **Syntactic ambiguity** mein confusion poore sentence ke structure se aata hai. <Cite id="ambiguity-challenges-gfg-ambiguity" /> <Cite id="ambiguity-challenges-tp-ambiguities" />

**Lexical example:** "I went to the **bank**" (paisa wala ya nadi wala?). Aur *book* noun bhi ho sakta hai ("read a book") aur verb bhi ("book a ticket"). <Cite id="ambiguity-challenges-wiki-polysemy" />

**Syntactic example:** "I saw the man with a telescope." Ya to maine telescope se usse dekha, ya us aadmi ke paas telescope tha. Words saaf hain; unka attach hona saaf nahi. <Cite id="ambiguity-challenges-wiki-synamb" />

| Point | Lexical ambiguity | Syntactic ambiguity |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Ek word ke kai meanings ya tags | Sentence ke kai possible structures |
| Also called | Word-level, semantic | Structural, grammatical |
| Cause | Homonymy, polysemy, noun/verb words | Phrase attachment, conjunction scope |
| Example | "bank", "bat" | "old men and women" |
| Parse trees | Aksar same tree, alag word meaning (noun/verb wala word tree badal sakta hai) | Do ya zyada alag trees |
| Resolved by | POS tagging, word sense disambiguation | Probabilistic parsing, context |

**Conclusion:** lexical ambiguity ye hai ki word ka matlab kya hai, syntactic ambiguity ye hai ki words kaise group hote hain; dono ko solve karne ke tools alag hain.

Exam mein English mein likho: "Lexical ambiguity arises from a single word having multiple meanings, whereas syntactic ambiguity arises from a sentence having multiple structures."
