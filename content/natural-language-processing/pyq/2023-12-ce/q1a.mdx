**In simple words:** a part-of-speech (POS) tagger gives each word a tag such as noun, verb or adjective. A **rule-based** tagger decides with hand-written rules. A **stochastic** tagger decides with probabilities counted from a tagged corpus. <Cite id="pos-tagging-slp3-ch18" /> <Cite id="pos-tagging-gfg-pos" />

**Rule-based tagger (for example ENGTWOL):**

1. Look every word up in a dictionary and list all its possible tags (*book*: noun or verb).
2. Apply hand-written rules that throw away tags that do not fit. Example rule: "if the previous word is a determiner (the, a), the word cannot be a verb."
3. Keep the tag that survives.

**Stochastic tagger (for example HMM tagger):** choose the tag sequence with the highest probability, using word-tag counts and tag-after-tag counts from a corpus. For "the book", the count of *the* followed by a noun is much higher than *the* followed by a verb, so *book* is tagged noun. <Cite id="pos-tagging-tp-pos" />

| Point | Rule-based | Stochastic |
|---|---|---|
| Knowledge from | Linguist-written rules | Tagged training corpus |
| Output | Tag that survives the rules | Most probable tag or sequence |
| Effort | Rules take long to write and maintain | Needs only a big tagged corpus |
| Weak point | Cannot handle cases no rule covers | Fails on unseen words and rare patterns |

A third kind, the **transformation-based** Brill tagger, starts with the most frequent tag and learns correction rules from data. <Cite id="pos-tagging-wiki-brill" />

**Conclusion:** rule-based taggers use knowledge written by experts; stochastic taggers learn the same knowledge from counted data.
