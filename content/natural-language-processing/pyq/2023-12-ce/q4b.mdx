**In simple words:** POS tagging gives each word a tag (noun, verb, adjective and so on). It looks easy, but a tagger meets several problems. The biggest is that one word can have many tags, and only the context tells which is right. <Cite id="pos-tagging-slp3-ch18" /> <Cite id="pos-tagging-gfg-pos" />

**1. Ambiguity (the main challenge).** Many common words have more than one tag. *Book* is a noun in "read a **book**" and a verb in "**book** a ticket". In the Brown corpus a large share of word tokens are ambiguous, although most word types are not. "Time flies like an arrow" can be tagged in several ways: *flies* as verb or noun, *like* as verb or preposition. <Cite id="pos-tagging-wiki-pos-tagging" />

**2. Unknown words.** Names, new words (*selfie*), slang, typing mistakes and technical terms are not in the training data, so the tagger has no counts for them. Clues such as capital letters, suffixes (*-ing*, *-ly*, *-tion*) and context are used.

**3. Multi-word expressions and idioms.** "in spite of", "New York", "kick the bucket" act as one unit, but a word-by-word tagger tags each word separately.

**4. Tag set and annotation problems.** Tag sets differ in size (45 tags in the Penn Treebank, 17 in Universal Dependencies) and a model trained on one cannot be used with another. Even human annotators disagree on hard cases, so the "gold" data has errors; accuracy is capped near 97 to 98 per cent. <Cite id="pos-tagging-tp-pos" />

**5. Data sparsity and domain change.** A tagger trained on news does badly on tweets, medical text or Hinglish. Rare word-tag pairs have too few counts.

**6. Long-distance context.** A simple HMM looks only at the previous one or two tags, but sometimes the clue is far away ("The horse raced past the barn fell").

**7. Languages with free word order or rich morphology** (Hindi, Turkish) have many word forms, so there is less data per form and word order gives fewer hints.

| Challenge | Example | Common remedy |
|---|---|---|
| Ambiguity | book (noun or verb) | Context, HMM, CRF |
| Unknown words | selfie | Suffix and capital-letter features |
| Multi-word units | in spite of | Treat as one token |
| Different tag sets | 45 vs 17 tags | Map between tag sets |
| Domain shift | news vs tweets | Train on matching data |

**Conclusion:** ambiguity, unknown words and limited context are the core challenges; better tagging uses context, word-shape features and training data from the same domain.
