**In simple words:** n-gram model us word sequence ko probability zero deta hai jo usne kabhi nahi dekhi, aur isse poora sentence bigad jaata hai. Smoothing seen sequences se thodi probability leti hai aur unseen ko deti hai. Good-Turing aisi ek method hai. <Cite id="class-nlp-m6" loc="slide 44" />

Class Good-Turing smoothing ka naam batati hai par formula nahi deti, isliye neeche ka idea standard wala hai.

- c baar dikhe words ko kam count milta hai $c^* = (c+1)\,N_{c+1}/N_c$, jahan $N_c$ un alag words ki sankhya hai jo exactly c baar dikhe.
- Unseen words ko milne wali total probability $N_1/N$ hai, jahan $N_1$ ek baar dikhe words ki sankhya hai aur $N$ tokens ki sankhya.

| Quantity | Example mein value |
|---|---|
| Ek, do, teen baar dikhe words ($N_1$, $N_2$, $N_3$) | 3, 1, 1 |
| Tokens $N = 3 \times 1 + 1 \times 2 + 1 \times 3$ | 8 |
| Saare unseen words ki probability $= N_1/N$ | 3/8 = 0.375 |
| Ek baar dikhe word ka adjusted count $= 2 \times N_2/N_1$ | 2 x 1/3 = 0.67 |

**Conclusion:** Good-Turing ek baar dikhe words ki sankhya se unseen words ka chance estimate karti hai, isliye kisi sequence ko probability zero nahi milti.
