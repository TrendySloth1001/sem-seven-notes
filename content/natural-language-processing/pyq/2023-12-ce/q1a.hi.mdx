**In simple words:** part-of-speech (POS) tagger har word ko ek tag deta hai, jaise noun, verb ya adjective. **Rule-based** tagger hand-written rules se decide karta hai. **Stochastic** tagger tagged corpus se gine hue probabilities se decide karta hai. <Cite id="pos-tagging-slp3-ch18" /> <Cite id="pos-tagging-gfg-pos" />

**Rule-based tagger (jaise ENGTWOL):**

1. Har word ko dictionary mein dekho aur uske saare possible tags likho (*book*: noun ya verb).
2. Hand-written rules lagao jo galat tags hata dete hain. Example rule: "agar pichla word determiner (the, a) hai, to ye word verb nahi ho sakta."
3. Jo tag bach jaye wahi rakho.

**Stochastic tagger (jaise HMM tagger):** corpus ke word-tag counts aur tag-ke-baad-tag counts use karke sabse zyada probability wali tag sequence chuno. "the book" mein *the* ke baad noun ka count *the* ke baad verb se bahut zyada hai, isliye *book* ko noun tag milta hai. <Cite id="pos-tagging-tp-pos" />

| Point | Rule-based | Stochastic |
|---|---|---|
| Knowledge from | Linguist-written rules | Tagged training corpus |
| Output | Tag that survives the rules | Most probable tag or sequence |
| Effort | Rules likhne aur maintain karne mein time lagta hai | Sirf bada tagged corpus chahiye |
| Weak point | Jo case kisi rule mein nahi, wahan fail | Unseen words aur rare patterns pe fail |

Teesra type, **transformation-based** Brill tagger, sabse frequent tag se shuru karta hai aur data se correction rules seekhta hai. <Cite id="pos-tagging-wiki-brill" />

**Conclusion:** rule-based taggers experts ka likha knowledge use karte hain; stochastic taggers wahi knowledge gine hue data se seekhte hain.
