**In simple words:** tokenization text ko chhote tukdon mein kaat deta hai jinhe tokens kehte hain (words ya sentences). Stemming phir har word ko ek rough root tak chhota karta hai, taaki milte-julte words match ho jayein. <Cite id="text-preprocessing-gfg-tok" /> <Cite id="porter-stemmer-gfg-stemming" />

**Tokenization**

- **Token** text ki ek unit hai, aam taur par word ya punctuation mark.
- Kyun chahiye: computer ko sirf characters ki lambi string dikhti hai. Words ginne, tag karne ya parse karne se pehle use word boundaries dhoondhni padti hain.
- Simple rule: spaces aur punctuation pe split karo. Mushkil cases: *don't* (do + n't?), *New York* (ek unit ya do?), *U.S.A.* (dots sentence ka end nahi hain). <Cite id="text-preprocessing-slp3-ch2" loc="§2.5" />

**Stemming**

- **Stem** word ka wo hissa hai jo suffix hatane ke baad bachta hai. Result real word ho, ye zaroori nahi.
- Kyun chahiye: search aur classification mein *run, runs, running* ek hi word ginne chahiye.
- Porter stemmer sabse mashhoor rule-based method hai. <Cite id="porter-stemmer-def" />

**Example** "The runners were running quickly" pe

| Step | Output |
|---|---|
| Tokens | The, runners, were, running, quickly |
| Lowercase aur stemming (Porter) ke baad | the, runner, were, run, quickli |

*running* se *run* bana aur *runners* se *runner*. *quickli* real word nahi hai, jisse dikhta hai ki stem sirf ek rough root hai.

**Conclusion:** tokenization words dhoondhta hai, stemming unke forms chhote karta hai, aur dono milke text ko aage ke NLP steps ke liye taiyaar karte hain.
