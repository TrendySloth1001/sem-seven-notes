**In simple words:** tokenization cuts text into words; stemming cuts the endings off words.

| Point | Tokenization | Stemming |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | Breaking a continuous stream of text into smaller, meaningful units called tokens (usually words) <Cite id="class-nlp-asol" loc="p. 11" /> | A simplified form of morphological analysis used to find the root word <Cite id="class-nlp-m2" loc="p. 19" /> |
| Works on | The whole text | Each word |
| Example | "NLP is fun" gives "NLP", "is", "fun" <Cite id="class-nlp-m2" loc="p. 18" /> | retrieval, retrieved, retrieving give retriev <Cite id="class-nlp-asol" loc="p. 12" /> |
| Note | Hard for hyphenated words and languages without spaces | Crude: the stem may not be a dictionary word |

**Conclusion:** first tokenize, then stem each token.
