**In simple words:** many words have more than one meaning (sense). **Word sense disambiguation (WSD)** is the task of choosing the correct sense of a word for the sentence it appears in. The computer looks at the neighbouring words, as a person does. <Cite id="word-sense-disambiguation-slp3-I" loc="§I.4" /> <Cite id="ambiguity-challenges-wiki-wsd" />

**Example.** The word *bank*:

- "He deposited money in the **bank**." means a financial institution.
- "They sat on the river **bank**." means the side of a river.

**Why it is needed:** machine translation (*bank* has different Hindi words for each sense), search engines, question answering and text classification all need the right sense. <Cite id="word-sense-disambiguation-tp-wsd" />

**Approaches**

1. **Knowledge-based:** use a dictionary such as WordNet. Lesk's algorithm picks the sense whose definition shares most words with the sentence.
2. **Supervised:** train a classifier on sentences already tagged with senses.
3. **Semi-supervised:** start from a few tagged examples and grow (Yarowsky).
4. **Unsupervised:** group similar contexts from raw text. <Cite id="word-sense-disambiguation-gfg-wsd" />

**Difficulty:** dictionaries split senses differently, and tagged data is costly to make.

**Conclusion:** WSD picks the one sense of an ambiguous word that fits the context.
