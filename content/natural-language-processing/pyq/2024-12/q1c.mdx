**In simple words:** discourse analysis joins the sentences of a text so that the whole thing makes sense. Pragmatic analysis works out what the speaker really means in the situation. <Cite id="class-nlp-asol" loc="p. 1" />

**Discourse Analysis** focuses on understanding how multiple sentences connect within a larger context. It ensures that the meaning of a text remains coherent and consistent across sentences and paragraphs. Key aspects: anaphora resolution and contextual references. *Example:* "Taylor went to the store. She bought groceries." Here *She* refers to *Taylor*.

**Pragmatic Analysis** focuses on understanding the intended meaning of words and sentences by considering context, tone, and speaker intention beyond the literal meaning. Key tasks: understanding intentions and figurative meaning (idioms, metaphors). It supports sentiment analysis and chatbots. *Example:* "Can you pass the salt?" is a polite request, not a question about ability.

| | Discourse analysis | Pragmatic analysis |
|---|---|---|
| Looks at | how sentences connect | what the speaker means |
| Example | *She* = *Taylor* | "Can you pass the salt?" = a request |

**Conclusion:** discourse analysis gives a coherent text; pragmatic analysis gives its real meaning.
