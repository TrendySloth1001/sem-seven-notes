**In simple words:** Named Entity Recognition (NER) finds the names of real things in text and labels their type, such as person, place, organisation or date. (The paper writes "resolution"; the standard name of this task is recognition.) <Cite id="pyq-2024-12-wiki-ner" />

**Example.** "Sundar Pichai visited Mumbai on 5 May 2024 to meet Tata Group."

| Text | Entity type |
|---|---|
| Sundar Pichai | PERSON |
| Mumbai | LOCATION |
| 5 May 2024 | DATE |
| Tata Group | ORGANISATION |

**Steps:** (1) tokenize the text and tag each word with its part of speech; (2) detect which words form an entity; (3) classify each entity into a type, using context. Context matters: "Amazon is expanding" is an organisation, "the Amazon is a rainforest" is a location. <Cite id="pyq-2024-12-gfg-ner" />

**Methods:** rule-based (dictionaries, patterns), machine learning (HMM, CRF) and deep learning (BiLSTM, BERT). Labels are often written in BIO form (B = begin, I = inside, O = outside an entity).

**Applications:** information extraction, search, question answering, news tagging, resume parsing. **Difficulties:** ambiguity of names, new names, spelling variants.

**Conclusion:** NER turns plain text into labelled names, a key step for information extraction.
