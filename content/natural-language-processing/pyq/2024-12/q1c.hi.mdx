**In simple words:** ek akela sentence kam hi poori baat batata hai. **Discourse analysis** dekhta hai ki sentences kaise jud kar meaningful text banate hain. **Pragmatic analysis** dekhta hai ki speaker situation mein asal mein kya kehna chahta hai, dictionary meaning se aage. Ye NLP mein understanding ke aakhri do levels hain. <Cite id="nlp-introduction-gfg-phases" />

**Discourse analysis.** Ek sentence ka meaning pichhle sentences par depend karta hai. Do cheezein dekhi jaati hain: **coherence** (kya sentences logically jude hain?) aur **reference** (*he*, *it* jaise pronouns kis ki taraf point karte hain?). <Cite id="discourse-reference-slp3-disc" /> <Cite id="discourse-reference-wiki-discourse" />

Example: "Ravi went to the shop. **He** bought milk." Discourse analysis *He* ko *Ravi* se jodta hai. Is link ke bina doosra sentence unclear hai.

**Pragmatic analysis.** Ye real-world knowledge, speaker ke intention aur situation se asli meaning nikalta hai. <Cite id="discourse-reference-wiki-pragmatics" />

Example: "Can you pass the salt?" Literal meaning ability ka sawaal hai, par asli meaning request hai. Ek aur: "It is cold here" ka matlab ho sakta hai "please window band karo".

| Point | Discourse analysis | Pragmatic analysis |
|---|---|---|
| Kya dekhta hai | Sentences ke beech links | Context mein meaning aur intention |
| Main problem | Pronoun aur reference resolution, coherence | Intended meaning, indirect requests |
| Example | He = Ravi | "Can you pass the salt?" = request |

**Conclusion:** discourse analysis sentences ko ek poore text mein jodta hai, aur pragmatic analysis tay karta hai ki speaker asal mein kya kehna chahta hai.
