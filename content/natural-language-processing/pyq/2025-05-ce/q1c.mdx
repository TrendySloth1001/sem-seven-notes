**In simple words:** both models tag words well but each has weak points. <Cite id="class-nlp-pyq3" loc="p. 5" /> <Cite id="class-nlp-pyq3" loc="p. 10" />

**Limitations of HMM** <Cite id="class-nlp-pyq3" loc="p. 5" />

1. **Strong independence assumption:** the next state depends only on the current state, and each word only on its own tag.
2. **Limited context:** it cannot capture long-range dependencies.
3. **Data sparsity:** it needs a large labelled corpus.
4. **Unseen words:** it cannot handle out-of-vocabulary words well.
5. **Generative model:** it is hard to add extra features (grammar, meaning).
6. **Slow** when the vocabulary or the number of states is large.

**Limitations of the Maximum Entropy model** <Cite id="class-nlp-pyq3" loc="p. 10" />

1. **Label bias problem:** probabilities are normalized locally at each step rather than over the whole sentence, so a high-probability move from a state with few outgoing choices can dominate decoding even when the whole sequence is unlikely.
2. **Computational cost:** training needs iterative numerical optimization (L-BFGS, GIS, IIS), which is heavier than the simple counting of an HMM.

| Model | Main weakness |
|---|---|
| HMM | Independence assumptions and no rich features |
| MaxEnt (MEMM) | Label bias and costly training |

**Conclusion:** HMM is limited by its assumptions, MaxEnt by label bias and training cost.
