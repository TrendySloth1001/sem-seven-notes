**In simple words:** a bigram model looks only at the previous word. Counting word pairs gives probabilities; the next word is the most probable one, and a sentence is better if the product of its probabilities is larger.

**Concepts and formula:** an N-gram is a continuous sequence of n words in a text; a bigram is a sequence of two words (unigram: one word; trigram: three). <Cite id="class-nlp-m2" loc="p. 8" /> <Cite id="class-nlp-pyq2" loc="p. 1" /> The bigram probability is $P(B \mid A) = \text{count}(AB)/\text{count}(A)$. <Cite id="class-nlp-m2" loc="p. 9" /> For n-grams in general, the next word depends on the previous n - 1 words.

<Sum>

<Given>

Corpus: `<s>` Sam I am `</s>`; `<s>` Sam I like `</s>`; `<s>` I am Sam `</s>`; `<s>` Sam I do like `</s>`; `<s>` do I like Sam `</s>`.

</Given>

<Find>

1. the most probable next word after (a) `<s>` Sam, (b) `<s>` Sam I do, (c) `<s>` Sam I am Sam, (d) `<s>` do I like; 2. which of (e) `<s>` Sam I do I like `</s>`, (f) `<s>` Sam I am `</s>`, (g) `<s>` I do like Sam I am `</s>` has the highest probability.

</Find>

<Steps>

1. **Unigram counts:** `<s>` 5, Sam 5, I 5, am 2, like 3, do 2, `</s>` 5 (27 tokens, 22 bigrams).
2. **Bigram probabilities:**

In the table, n(AB) = count(AB), n(A) = count(A) and P = P(B given A).

| Bigram AB | n(AB) | n(A) | P |
|---|---|---|---|
| `<s>` Sam | 3 | 5 | 3/5 = 0.60 |
| Sam I | 3 | 5 | 3/5 = 0.60 |
| I am | 2 | 5 | 2/5 = 0.40 |
| am `</s>` | 1 | 2 | 1/2 = 0.50 |
| I like | 2 | 5 | 2/5 = 0.40 |
| like `</s>` | 2 | 3 | 2/3 = 0.67 |
| `<s>` I | 1 | 5 | 1/5 = 0.20 |
| am Sam | 1 | 2 | 1/2 = 0.50 |
| Sam `</s>` | 2 | 5 | 2/5 = 0.40 |
| I do | 1 | 5 | 1/5 = 0.20 |
| do like | 1 | 2 | 1/2 = 0.50 |
| `<s>` do | 1 | 5 | 1/5 = 0.20 |
| do I | 1 | 2 | 1/2 = 0.50 |
| like Sam | 1 | 3 | 1/3 = 0.33 |

3. **Next words:** (a) after Sam: I 3/5 = 0.60 against `</s>` 2/5, so **I**. (b) after do: like 1/2 and I 1/2 are tied, so **like or I**. (c) after Sam: again **I** (0.60). (d) after like: `</s>` 2/3 = 0.67 against Sam 1/3, so **`</s>`**.
4. **(e)** $P = 3/5 \times 3/5 \times 1/5 \times 1/2 \times 2/5 \times 2/3 = 6/625 = 0.0096$.
5. **(f)** $P = 3/5 \times 3/5 \times 2/5 \times 1/2 = 9/125 = 0.072$.
6. **(g)** $P = 1/5 \times 1/5 \times 1/2 \times 1/3 \times 3/5 \times 2/5 \times 1/2 = 1/1250 = 0.0008$.

</Steps>

<Answer>

Next words: (a) I, (b) like or I (tie), (c) I, (d) `</s>`. The best sentence is **(f) `<s>` Sam I am `</s>`** with probability 0.072.

</Answer>

<Box kind="verify">

Each group of next-word probabilities adds to 1 (after Sam 3/5 + 2/5; after do 1/2 + 1/2; after like 2/3 + 1/3). Multiplying (f) in decimals: 0.6 x 0.6 x 0.4 x 0.5 = 0.072. The ordering 0.072 > 0.0096 > 0.0008 shows that longer sentences with rare pairs score lower.

</Box>

</Sum>

The product rule for a whole sentence is standard; the class files work only the next-word case.

**Conclusion:** the bigram model predicts the next word from counts, and ranks sentences by the product of their bigram probabilities.
