**In simple words:** an **n-gram** is a run of $n$ words in a row. A **bigram** ($n = 2$) is a pair of neighbouring words. A language model uses counts of these runs to say how likely a sentence is. <Cite id="ngram-language-models-slp3-ch3" /> <Cite id="ngram-language-models-gfg-ngram" /> <Cite id="ngram-language-models-wiki-ngram" />

**Why:** by the chain rule, $P(w_1 \dots w_k) = \prod_i P(w_i \mid w_1 \dots w_{i-1})$, but long histories never occur in a corpus. The **Markov assumption** keeps only the last $n-1$ words.

- **N-gram:** $P(w_i \mid w_{i-n+1} \dots w_{i-1}) = \dfrac{C(w_{i-n+1} \dots w_i)}{C(w_{i-n+1} \dots w_{i-1})}$
- **Bigram:** $P(w_i \mid w_{i-1}) = \dfrac{C(w_{i-1}\, w_i)}{C(w_{i-1})}$, and $P(\text{sentence}) = \prod_i P(w_i \mid w_{i-1})$.

Here $C$ is a count in the corpus. Unigram, bigram and trigram are $n = 1, 2, 3$.

<Sum>

<Given>

Five sentences, each starting with `<s>` and ending with `</s>`: (1) Sam I am (2) Sam I like (3) I am Sam (4) Sam I do like (5) do I like Sam.

</Given>

<Find>

the likely next words for (a) to (d), and the probabilities of sentences (e), (f), (g).

</Find>

<Steps>

1. **Counts.** `<s>`=5 (I 1, Sam 3, do 1). Sam=5 (I 3, `</s>` 2). I=5 (am 2, like 2, do 1). am=2 (Sam 1, `</s>` 1). like=3 (`</s>` 2, Sam 1). do=2 (like 1, I 1). *Reason: bigram probability is pair count over first-word count.*
2. **(a)** after `<s> Sam` the model looks at Sam: I = 3/5 is highest. Next word: **I**.
3. **(b)** after `do`: like = 1/2 and I = 1/2, a tie. Next word: **like or I** (equally likely).
4. **(c)** after `Sam`: **I** (3/5), the same as (a). *Reason: a bigram model looks only at the last word.*
5. **(d)** after `like`: `</s>` = 2/3 against Sam = 1/3. Next: **`</s>`** (sentence ends).
6. **(e)** `<s> Sam I do I like </s>` $= \frac{3}{5}\cdot\frac{3}{5}\cdot\frac{1}{5}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{2}{5}\cdot\frac{2}{3} = \frac{36}{3750} = 0.0096$.
7. **(f)** `<s> Sam I am </s>` $= \frac{3}{5}\cdot\frac{3}{5}\cdot\frac{2}{5}\cdot\frac{1}{2} = \frac{18}{250} = 0.072$.
8. **(g)** `<s> I do like Sam I am </s>` $= \frac{1}{5}\cdot\frac{1}{5}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{3}{5}\cdot\frac{2}{5}\cdot\frac{1}{2} = \frac{6}{7500} = 0.0008$.

</Steps>

<Answer>

(a) I, (b) like or I (tie), (c) I, (d) `</s>`. Probabilities: (e) 0.0096, (f) 0.072, (g) 0.0008, so **(f) is the best sentence**.

</Answer>

<Box kind="verify">

Check 1: each history's probabilities add to 1: `<s>` $\frac15 + \frac35 + \frac15 = 1$, Sam $\frac35 + \frac25 = 1$, I $\frac25 + \frac25 + \frac15 = 1$.

Check 2: (e) in decimals: $0.6 \times 0.6 = 0.36$, $\times 0.2 = 0.072$, $\times 0.5 = 0.036$, $\times 0.4 = 0.0144$, $\times 0.667 = 0.0096$, the same.

Check 3: (f) is a short sentence made of common pairs, (g) is long with rare pairs (`<s> I`, `I do`), so ordering (f) > (e) > (g) is sensible.

</Box>

</Sum>

**Limit:** an unseen pair gets probability 0, so smoothing is needed.

**Conclusion:** bigram probability is a count ratio; the best sentence is the one with the highest product, here (f).
