**In simple words:** morphological analysis (morphological parsing) ek word ko jaise likha hai waise (**surface form**) leta hai aur uska **stem** (lemma) plus **features** deta hai, jaise noun ya verb, singular ya plural, tense. Example: `cats` ka output `cat +N +PL`. <Cite id="morphology-fst-wiki-parsing" /> <Cite id="morphology-fst-gfg-morph" />

**Kaise hota hai:** finite state transducer ek **lexicon** (stems aur unki class) aur **rules** (spelling changes, jaise `fox` + `s` se `foxes`) use karta hai. Regular words affix hatakar mil jaate hain. Irregular words lexicon mein special case ki tarah store hote hain. <Cite id="morphology-fst-jm-ch3-notes" />

| Word type | Input (surface) | Output (stem + features) |
|---|---|---|
| Regular verb | `walked` | `walk +V +PAST` |
| Regular verb | `walks` | `walk +V +3SG` |
| Regular verb | `walking` | `walk +V +PRES-PART` |
| Irregular verb | `went` | `go +V +PAST` |
| Irregular verb | `sang` | `sing +V +PAST` |
| Irregular verb | `sung` | `sing +V +PAST-PART` |
| Singular noun | `cat` | `cat +N +SG` |
| Singular noun | `goose` | `goose +N +SG` |
| Plural noun | `cats` | `cat +N +PL` |
| Plural noun | `foxes` | `fox +N +PL` |
| Plural noun (irregular) | `mice` | `mouse +N +PL` |

**Regular vs irregular:** `walked` ko `walk` + `-ed` mein todte hain, to rule general hai. `went` mein `-ed` dikhta hi nahi, isliye analyser ko irregular forms ki list mein dekhna padta hai. <Cite id="morphology-fst-wiki-irregular" />

**Note:** kuch forms ambiguous hain (`saw` matlab `see +V +PAST` ya `saw +N +SG`), to choose karne ke liye tagger lag sakta hai.

**Conclusion:** output hamesha stem plus grammar tags hota hai: regular words rules se, irregular words lexicon se.

Exam mein English mein likho: "Morphological analysis maps a surface word to its stem and grammatical features, for example cats gives cat +N +PL."
