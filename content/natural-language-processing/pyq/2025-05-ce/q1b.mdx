**In simple words:** morphological analysis (morphological parsing) takes a word as written (the **surface form**) and returns its **stem** (lemma) plus **features** such as noun or verb, singular or plural, tense. Example: `cats` gives `cat +N +PL`. <Cite id="morphology-fst-wiki-parsing" /> <Cite id="morphology-fst-gfg-morph" />

**How it is done:** a finite state transducer uses a **lexicon** (stems and their classes) and **rules** (spelling changes such as `fox` + `s` becoming `foxes`). Regular words are found by removing the affix. Irregular words are stored in the lexicon as special cases. <Cite id="morphology-fst-jm-ch3-notes" />

| Word type | Input (surface) | Output (stem + features) |
|---|---|---|
| Regular verb | `walked` | `walk +V +PAST` |
| Regular verb | `walks` | `walk +V +3SG` |
| Regular verb | `walking` | `walk +V +PRES-PART` |
| Irregular verb | `went` | `go +V +PAST` |
| Irregular verb | `sang` | `sing +V +PAST` |
| Irregular verb | `sung` | `sing +V +PAST-PART` |
| Singular noun | `cat` | `cat +N +SG` |
| Singular noun | `goose` | `goose +N +SG` |
| Plural noun | `cats` | `cat +N +PL` |
| Plural noun | `foxes` | `fox +N +PL` |
| Plural noun (irregular) | `mice` | `mouse +N +PL` |

**Regular versus irregular:** `walked` is split as `walk` + `-ed`, so the rule is general. `went` has no visible `-ed`, so the analyser must look it up in a list of irregular forms. <Cite id="morphology-fst-wiki-irregular" />

**Note:** some forms are ambiguous (`saw` is `see +V +PAST` or `saw +N +SG`), so a tagger may be needed to choose.

**Conclusion:** the output is always stem plus grammar tags: regular words come from rules, irregular words from the lexicon.
