**In simple words:** people do not repeat names. They say *he*, *it*, *the girl*. **Reference resolution** is finding which real thing (entity) each such phrase points to. <Cite id="discourse-reference-slp3-coref" />

**Key terms:** a **referring expression** is a phrase that points to something (*she*, *the girl*, *Priya*). The **referent** is the thing it points to. In *Priya lost her bag*, *her* is the **anaphor** and *Priya* is its **antecedent**. Two phrases that point to one entity **corefer**. <Cite id="discourse-reference-wiki-anaphora" /> <Cite id="discourse-reference-wiki-coref" />

**Kinds of referring expressions:** pronouns (*she*), definite noun phrases (*the girl*), indefinite noun phrases (*a girl*, which introduces a new entity), proper names (*Priya*), demonstratives (*this book*).

**Why it is a problem:** in *Priya met Anita. She gave her a book.* both women fit *she* and *her*. A word like *it* in *It is raining* points to nothing. Sometimes only world knowledge helps: *The trophy did not fit in the suitcase because it was too big* (*it* is the trophy).

**How the problem is solved:**

1. Collect candidate antecedents from the earlier text.
2. Remove impossible ones using **constraints**: agreement in number, gender and person; syntactic rules (*Jill likes him* means *him* is not Jill); meaning of the verb (*ate it* means *it* is food).
3. Rank the rest using **preferences**: recent mention, subject over object, same role in parallel sentences.
4. Pick the best. Methods: Hobbs' tree-walk, Centering, or machine learning. <Cite id="anaphora-resolution-tp-nlp" />

**Conclusion:** reference resolution links every pronoun and noun phrase to its entity using constraints first and preferences next; it is needed for translation, summarisation and question answering.
