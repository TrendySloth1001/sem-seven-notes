**In simple words:** an affix is a small piece added to a word to change its meaning or its grammar. The main word is the **stem** (root), for example `play`. `un-`, `-ing` and `-er` are affixes. An affix cannot stand alone as a word, so it is a **bound morpheme**. <Cite id="morphology-fst-wiki-affix" /> <Cite id="morphology-fst-gfg-morph" />

**Why it matters:** NLP programs split words into stem and affixes (morphological analysis), so that `played`, `playing` and `player` are all linked to `play`.

**Types of affixes:**

| Type | Where it goes | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Prefix | Before the stem | `un-happy`, `re-write`, `dis-agree` <Cite id="morphology-fst-wiki-prefix" /> |
| Suffix | After the stem | `play-ing`, `kind-ness`, `walk-ed` <Cite id="morphology-fst-wiki-suffix" /> |
| Infix | Inside the stem | Tagalog `sulat` (write) becomes `s-um-ulat` (wrote). English has only jokey cases such as `abso-bloody-lutely` |
| Circumfix | Around the stem, on both sides | German `lieb` (love) becomes `ge-lieb-t` (loved) |

English uses mainly prefixes and suffixes. Many other languages use infixes and circumfixes.

**By job:** an affix can be *inflectional* (only grammar, same word class: `cat` to `cats`) or *derivational* (builds a new word, often a new class: `happy` to `happiness`).

**Conclusion:** affixes are the bound parts attached to a stem as prefix, suffix, infix or circumfix; stemmers and morphological analysers work by finding and removing them.
