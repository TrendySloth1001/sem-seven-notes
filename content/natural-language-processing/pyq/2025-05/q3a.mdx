**In simple words:** a **stemmer** cuts the ending off a word so that `connected`, `connecting` and `connection` all become `connect`. The Porter stemmer (Martin Porter, 1980) does this with a fixed list of suffix-stripping rules applied in five steps. The result is a stem, which need not be a real word. <Cite id="porter-stemmer-def" /> <Cite id="porter-stemmer-gfg-stemming" />

**Why it is needed:** search engines and classifiers treat `play`, `plays` and `playing` as one idea. Stemming shrinks the vocabulary and raises recall.

**Key idea: the measure m.** Write a word as `[C](VC)^m[V]`, where C is a run of consonants and V a run of vowels (`y` counts as a vowel after a consonant). `m` counts the vowel-consonant groups. `tree` has m=0, `trouble` m=1, `troubles` m=2. A rule fires only if the stem left over meets a condition, such as `m>1` (stem is long enough), `*v*` (stem has a vowel) or `*d` (ends in a double consonant). This stops `bed` being cut to `b`. <Cite id="porter-stemmer-def" />

```mermaid
%% caption: The five steps of Porter's algorithm run in order. Each step only applies the longest matching suffix rule whose condition holds.
flowchart LR
  A["Word"] --> B["1a: plurals (sses to ss, ies to i, s to nothing)"]
  B --> C["1b: ed and ing, then clean-up"]
  C --> D["1c: y to i"]
  D --> E["2: long suffixes to short (ational to ate)"]
  E --> F["3: ical, ful, ness, ative"]
  F --> G["4: delete suffixes if m>1"]
  G --> H["5: final e and double l"]
  H --> I["Stem"]
```

**The steps:**

| Step | Job | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1a | Plurals | `caresses` to `caress`, `ponies` to `poni` |
| 1b | `-eed`, `-ed`, `-ing` (stem needs a vowel), then fix-up (`at` to `ate`, double letter to single) | `hopping` to `hop`, `agreed` to `agree` |
| 1c | `y` to `i` if the stem has a vowel | `happy` to `happi` |
| 2 | Map double suffixes (needs m>0) | `relational` to `relate`, `generalization` to `generalize` |
| 3 | Remove `-ful`, `-ness`, `-ical`, `-ative` and similar (m>0) | `hopeful` to `hope` |
| 4 | Delete `-al`, `-ance`, `-ate`, `-ment`, `-ous`, `-ize` and similar (m>1) | `adjustment` to `adjust` |
| 5 | Drop final `e` (m>1), reduce `ll` (m>1) | `probate` to `probat` |

<Sum>

<Given>

the words `relational` and `generalizations`.

</Given>

<Find>

the Porter stem of each.

</Find>

<Steps>

1. **relational.** Steps 1a to 1c do nothing (no `s`, `ed`, `ing`, `y`). Step 2: `ational` becomes `ate`, stem `rel` has m=1 (>0), giving `relate`. *Reason: step 2 maps long suffixes to short ones.* Step 4: `ate` needs m>1 but `rel` has m=1, so it stays. Step 5: remove final `e` if m>1; `relat` has m=2, so `relat`.
2. **generalizations.** Step 1a: delete `s`, giving `generalization`. Step 2: `ization` becomes `ize` (stem `general`, m=3), giving `generalize`. Step 3: `alize` becomes `al` (stem `gener`, m=2), giving `general`. Step 4: delete `al` because `gener` has m=2>1, giving `gener`. Step 5: no `e`. *Reason: each step shortens the longest matching suffix.*

</Steps>

<Answer>

`relational` becomes `relat`; `generalizations` becomes `gener`.

</Answer>

<Box kind="verify">

Check m: `rel` = C V C, so m=1; `relat` = C V C V C, so m=2; `gener` = C V C V C, so m=2. The path generalizations, generalization, generalize, general, gener is the same one given in Porter's paper.

</Box>

</Sum>

**Advantages:** fast, simple, no dictionary. **Limitations:** over-stemming (`university` and `universe` both give `univers`), under-stemming (`data`, `datum`), stems that are not words (`poni`), English only. Snowball (Porter2) improves it. <Cite id="porter-stemmer-snowball" />

**Conclusion:** Porter's stemmer strips suffixes in five rule-based steps controlled by the measure m.
