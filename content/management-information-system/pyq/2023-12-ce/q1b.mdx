**In simple words:** every year it gets easier to attack a firm's information and harder to protect it. Rainer and Prince name five reasons. <Cite id="is-fundamentals-wiley-rainer" />

**Vulnerability** means a weakness that a threat can use to harm an information resource. <Cite id="note-information-security-wiki-cybercrime" />

| # | Factor | Why it raises risk | Specific example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Interconnected, interdependent, wirelessly networked business environment | Every connected device or partner is a possible door for an attacker | A shop's payment system is linked to the Internet; a weak supplier link lets attackers in |
| 2 | Smaller, faster, cheaper computers and storage devices | A huge amount of data fits in a pen drive or phone, so it is easy to lose or steal | A laptop holding customer records is stolen from a car |
| 3 | Decreasing skills needed to be a hacker | Ready-made attack tools are free online, so a beginner can attack | A teenager runs a downloaded password-cracking tool |
| 4 | International organized crime taking over cybercrime | Well-funded gangs attack for profit and are hard to trace across borders | A gang locks a hospital's files with ransomware and demands payment |
| 5 | Lack of management support | Without budget and policy, basic protections are skipped | Management delays a patch, and the server is breached; WannaCry (2017) hit many systems that had not installed an available Windows patch <Cite id="note-information-security-wiki-wannacry" /> |

<Cite id="note-information-security-gfg-cybercrime" />

**Other reasons often added:** cloud and mobile use, remote work and insiders with too much access.

**Conclusion:** more connection, smaller devices, easier tools, organized criminals and weak management together mean information resources are more exposed every year, so layered controls are needed.
