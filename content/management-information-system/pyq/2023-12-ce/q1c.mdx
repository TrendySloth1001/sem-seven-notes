**In simple words:** business intelligence (BI) turns a firm's scattered data into reports and dashboards, so managers decide using facts instead of guesses.

**Definition:** BI is a set of applications, technologies and processes for collecting, storing, accessing and analysing data to help business users make better decisions. <Cite id="business-intelligence-wiki-bi" /> <Cite id="business-intelligence-ibm-bi" />

**How BI reaches the decision:** source systems (TPS, ERP, CRM) feed ETL (extract, clean, load), which fills a data warehouse; tools such as OLAP and data mining analyse it; dashboards present it; the manager decides. <Cite id="business-intelligence-gfg-bi" />

**Impact on decision making**

1. **Fact-based decisions:** data replaces intuition.
2. **Faster decisions:** data is already integrated, so answers come in minutes, not weeks.
3. **Single view of the business:** sales, finance and stock are seen together.
4. **Better forecasting:** trends help plan demand and budgets.
5. **Early warning:** dashboards flag falling sales or rising cost early.
6. **Customer understanding:** data mining shows buying patterns for targeted offers.
7. **Decisions at every level:** self-service BI helps operational staff, not only executives.
8. **Measurement:** KPIs show whether a decision worked. <Cite id="business-intelligence-tp-bi" />

**Example:** a retail chain's dashboard shows winter jacket sales in North India rising two weeks early. The manager moves stock from southern stores at once and avoids shortage.

**Limits:** poor data quality gives wrong answers, cost is high, too many dashboards cause overload, and numbers can ignore human judgment.

**Conclusion:** BI makes decisions faster, better informed and measurable, provided the data is clean and managers still use judgment.
