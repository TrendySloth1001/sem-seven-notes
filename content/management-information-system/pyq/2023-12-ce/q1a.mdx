**In simple words:** knowledge management (KM) means collecting what a firm's people know and sharing it so others can use it. It sounds easy, but many KM projects fail, mostly because of people and culture, not technology.

**Definition:** knowledge management is the set of processes and tools used to create, capture, store, share and use an organization's knowledge, so the right person gets the right knowledge at the right time. <Cite id="knowledge-management-ibm" />

**Challenges of KM**

1. **Unwillingness to share:** employees think "knowledge is power" and fear losing their value if they share. <Cite id="knowledge-management-tp-challenges" />
2. **Tacit knowledge is hard to capture:** experts often cannot explain what they "just know". <Cite id="knowledge-management-ibm" />
3. **Outdated or wrong content:** old content makes people stop trusting the repository.
4. **Security versus sharing:** sensitive designs and client data must be restricted, yet KM wants wide sharing.
5. **Measuring value:** it is hard to put a number on knowledge, so it is hard to prove the project paid off. <Cite id="knowledge-management-tp-challenges" />
6. **Adoption and technology change:** tools work only if people use them, and tools keep changing.
7. **Information overload:** too much stored content makes the right item hard to find.

**Challenges in different businesses**

| Business | Typical KM challenge |
|---|---|
| Manufacturing | Shop-floor skills are tacit and held by senior workers near retirement |
| IT and consulting (Infosys, TCS) | Project knowledge is scattered and lost when staff move or leave |
| Banking (SBI) | Confidentiality and regulation limit what can be shared |
| Healthcare | Clinical guidance changes fast; patient privacy limits sharing |
| Multinational firms | Languages, cultures and time zones slow sharing |

<Cite id="knowledge-management-wiki-km" /> <Cite id="knowledge-management-rainer-mis" />

**Conclusion:** most KM problems come back to one question, why would a busy person spend time sharing what they know? Culture, rewards and easy tools answer it.
