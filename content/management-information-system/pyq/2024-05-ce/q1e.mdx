**In simple words:** an online shop looks simple to the customer, but behind the screen many records must be kept right: products, stock, payments, delivery and customers. The MIS collects, stores and reports this data, and connects the website to the warehouse, accounts and delivery. <Cite id="e-commerce-rainer-intro-is" /> <Cite id="e-commerce-laudon-mis" />

**How MIS supports e-commerce**

| Business need | What the MIS does |
|---|---|
| Show products | Product database with price, photos, stock and reviews |
| Take orders and payment | Records the order, links to the payment gateway, avoids double payment |
| Manage stock | Tracks every item in every warehouse; warns when stock is low |
| Deliver | Picks the nearest warehouse and courier; gives tracking |
| Know the customer | Customer data gives history, offers and recommendations |
| Decide | Reports: sales by day, returns, late deliveries |

**Case: a Flipkart-style shop**

1. The customer searches for a phone. The product database shows price and live stock, and the recommendation system suggests accessories.
2. The customer pays by UPI. The system records the order and the payment.
3. The inventory system reduces stock and picks the nearest warehouse. Low stock raises a purchase order to the supplier.
4. The logistics system assigns a courier and sends tracking updates.
5. Reports show the manager the best sellers and late deliveries, so action can be taken.

*Result:* faster delivery, fewer stock-outs and better decisions. Amazon works the same way on a bigger scale. <Cite id="e-commerce-ibm" />

**Conclusion:** MIS is the backbone that links the customer-facing site to stock, payment, delivery and management reports. <Cite id="e-commerce-gfg-intro" />
