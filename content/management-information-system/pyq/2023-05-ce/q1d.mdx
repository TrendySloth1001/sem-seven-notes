**In simple words:** a company's social media account is now the complaint desk that the whole world can see. A quick, polite public reply tells every other customer that the firm cares. Social computing means using web tools such as social networks, chat apps and forums for people to interact and share. <Cite id="social-computing-wiki-social-computing" />

**How social computing is changing customer service**

1. **Fast, public support:** customers post a problem and the firm replies. Indian Railways passengers raise train and coach issues on X and the railway service accounts answer.
2. **24 x 7 help with chatbots:** bots on WhatsApp, Messenger and websites answer simple questions such as order status. SBI offers WhatsApp banking for balance and mini-statement requests. <Cite id="crm-wiki-chatbot" />
3. **Customer communities and forums:** other customers answer questions, which reduces the load on the support team.
4. **Social listening:** the firm searches for its brand name and finds complaints even when nobody tags it.
5. **Self-help content:** FAQs, how-to videos and wikis let customers solve problems themselves.
6. **Feedback loop:** reviews and complaints show what to fix in the product.
7. **Personal touch and CRM link:** staff can see earlier posts and answer in a friendly way, and a complaint becomes a service ticket in the CRM system (social CRM). <Cite id="social-computing-wiki-social-crm" />

| Benefit | Risk |
|---|---|
| Quick, low-cost support | A bad answer is seen by everyone |
| Spots problems early | Customers expect instant replies |
| Builds trust and loyalty | Large message volume is hard to handle |

**Example:** Zomato and Amazon answer complaints on social media and chat, and use the feedback to improve service. <Cite id="crm-gfg-intro" />

**Conclusion:** social computing turns customer service into an open, two-way conversation, which improves loyalty if the firm replies fast and well.
