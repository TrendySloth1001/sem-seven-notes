**In simple words:** a computer crime is a crime where a computer or network is the tool, the target or the place of the crime, such as hacking, online fraud or spreading viruses. To analyse the reasons means to ask: why do these crimes keep happening? <Cite id="ethics-privacy-wiki-cybercrime" />

**Definition:** computer crime (cybercrime) is any illegal act that uses a computer or network as the tool, the target or the place of the crime. <Cite id="note-information-security-gfg-cybercrime" />

**Main reasons** <Cite id="ethics-privacy-gfg-cybercrime-causes" /> <Cite id="ethics-privacy-gfg-cyber-criminals" />

| Reason | Why it encourages crime | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Financial gain | Fraud, ransomware and card theft pay well; the biggest motive | Ransomware demanding payment |
| Weak systems | Unpatched software, default or weak passwords give easy entry | Server not updated for a known bug |
| Lack of awareness | Users click links, reuse passwords, share OTPs | Employee enters password on a fake bank page |
| Easy copying of data | Digital data is copied in seconds and the original looks unchanged | Customer database copied to a pen drive |
| Anonymity and borders | Attackers hide behind fake identities in other countries; tracing is hard | Attacker in another country |
| Weak laws and under-reporting | Low chance of being caught or punished | Victims do not report the loss |
| Insiders | Employees already have access; greed or revenge | Disgruntled staff leak records |
| Thrill, ego, politics | Some attack to show skill, spy or protest | Hacktivist defaces a website |
| Fast technology change | New tools appear before defences catch up | Voice-cloning scam |
| Digital growth | More money, people and data online means more targets | UPI and e-commerce fraud |

**Common types:** phishing, identity theft, ransomware, hacking, denial-of-service, cyberstalking and piracy. <Cite id="note-information-security-gfg-cybercrime" />

**Indian law:** the Information Technology Act, 2000 punishes hacking (section 66), identity theft (66C), cheating by personation using a computer (66D) and cyber terrorism (66F). Incidents are reported to the cyber crime portal and CERT-In. <Cite id="note-information-security-wiki-itact" />

**Ways to reduce it:** patch software, use strong passwords and OTP, train users, take backups, encrypt data and monitor systems.

**Conclusion:** computer crime grows where gain is high, defences are weak, users are careless and the chance of punishment is low. Fixing these four causes reduces it.
