**In simple words:** an Executive Support System (ESS) shows the top bosses of a company the few most important figures on one easy screen. They can see how the company is doing and take big decisions. It is like the display in a pilot's cockpit: a few clear dials, and you can look closer at any one. <Cite id="is-fundamentals-tp-ess" />

**Definition:** an ESS (also called an Executive Information System) is a computer-based system that gives senior executives quick, easy access to summarised internal and external information, so they can monitor the organization and take strategic decisions. <Cite id="is-fundamentals-wiki-eis" />

**Features**

1. **Built for top management:** supports strategic, unstructured decisions.
2. **Easy to use:** graphs, menus and a dashboard; no computer skill needed.
3. **Drill-down:** click a total and go down to region, branch and single figures.
4. **Key indicators:** shows summarised figures such as revenue, profit and market share instead of long reports.
5. **Internal and external data:** joins company summaries with market news, competitor and government information.
6. **Trend analysis:** compares figures over time to spot problems early.
7. **Communication support:** email, alerts and scheduling tools.
8. **Little modelling:** it is for watching the business, not for heavy what-if analysis (that is a DSS).

**Example:** the CEO of a retail chain sees today's company sales on one screen, clicks the weakest city, then the weakest store, and finds the cause. <Cite id="business-intelligence-tp-ess" />

**Limits:** costly, hard to measure its benefit, and the data must always be current.

**Conclusion:** an ESS turns data into a simple, top-level picture for strategic control.
