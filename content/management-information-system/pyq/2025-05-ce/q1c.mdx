**In simple words:** topology is the shape of a network: how the computers and devices are arranged and joined to each other. It is like the road map of a city, which decides how traffic can move. <Cite id="networks-wireless-wiki-topology" />

**Definition:** network topology is the arrangement of the devices (nodes) and links of a network. It can be physical (how cables are laid) or logical (how data flows). <Cite id="networks-wireless-ibm-topology" />

**Types, with advantages and disadvantages** <Cite id="networks-wireless-gfg-topology" /> <Cite id="networks-wireless-tp-topologies" />

| Type | How it is connected | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bus | All devices on one shared main cable | Cheap; little cable | One cable break stops all; slow with many users |
| Star | Every device linked to a central switch | Easy to add devices; one bad cable affects one device | Switch failure stops everything |
| Ring | Closed loop; each device has two neighbours | Orderly data flow; equal access | One break can stop the ring; hard to add devices |
| Mesh | Devices linked to many or all others | Very reliable; many paths | Very costly; many cables |
| Tree | Stars joined to a parent in levels | Easy to grow in groups | Depends on the root device |
| Hybrid | Two or more types combined | Flexible; fits the need | Complex and costly |

**Use:** a college lab usually uses a star network (one switch per room); the internet backbone uses a partial mesh for reliability.

**Conclusion:** the choice depends on cost, reliability and size; star is the most common in offices.
