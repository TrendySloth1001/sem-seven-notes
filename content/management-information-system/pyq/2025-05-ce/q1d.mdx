**In simple words:** system development is the work of building a new information system, or changing an old one, so that it solves a real business need. Like building a house, it needs a plan, a design, construction, checking and handover. <Cite id="note-sdlc-gfg-sdlc" />

**Definition:** system development is the process of creating or changing an information system to meet the needs of the business. The System Development Life Cycle (SDLC) is the traditional, step-by-step structure for doing it. <Cite id="note-sdlc-wiki-sdlc" />

**Why a planned method is needed:** without a plan, projects finish late, cost more, or build something nobody wanted. <Cite id="note-sdlc-ibm-sdlc" />

**The six phases of the SDLC** <Cite id="it-impact-society-rainer-iis7" />

1. **Systems investigation:** why is the system needed, and is it feasible?
2. **Systems analysis:** what must the system do?
3. **Systems design:** how will it do it?
4. **Programming and testing:** build the system and check it.
5. **Implementation:** go live, convert from the old system, train users.
6. **Operation and maintenance:** run it, fix errors and improve it.

**How the system is acquired:** buy a ready package (Tally, SAP), rent it as SaaS (Salesforce), build in-house, outsource to a vendor (Infosys, TCS), use open source, or let end users build small tools.

**Who takes part:** users, managers, systems analysts (the bridge between business and technical people), programmers, testers and a project manager.

**Conclusion:** system development, organised through the SDLC, makes sure the right system is built on time and within cost.
