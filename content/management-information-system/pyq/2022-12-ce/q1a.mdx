**In simple words:** an MIS is a system that turns the everyday data of a business into short, useful reports for managers. MIS can be grouped in three common ways: by the management level it serves, by the kind of report it gives, and by the department it serves. <Cite id="is-fundamentals-gfg-mis" />

**Definition:** a Management Information System (MIS) is a planned system for collecting, storing and sending information so that managers can plan, control and decide. <Cite id="is-fundamentals-tp-mis" />

**Types of information systems by management level** (the usual textbook answer)

| Type | Used by | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Transaction processing system (TPS) | Clerks, operational staff | Records daily transactions such as bills and bookings |
| Management information system (MIS) | Middle managers | Summary and exception reports for control |
| Decision support system (DSS) | Middle and senior managers | Answers "what if" questions with models |
| Executive support system (ESS) | Senior executives | Dashboard of key figures for long-term decisions |

**Types of MIS by the report they give** <Cite id="is-fundamentals-gfg-types" />

| Type | What it gives | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Periodic (scheduled) report | Produced at fixed times in a fixed format | Monthly sales by region |
| Exception report | Only the unusual cases that need action | Items whose stock is below the reorder level |
| Demand (on-demand) report | Produced when a manager asks for it | Today's sales of one product |
| Ad-hoc report | A new, one-time report for a special question | Umbrella sales during last year's monsoon |

**Types of MIS by function (department)**

1. **Finance and accounting MIS:** budgets, cash flow, profit reports.
2. **Marketing MIS:** sales trends, customer and market reports.
3. **Human resource MIS:** attendance, payroll, staff turnover.
4. **Production and operations MIS:** stock levels, production schedule, quality reports.

Each of these is a subsystem of the whole MIS and feeds from the same central database. <Cite id="is-fundamentals-wiki-mis" />

**Example:** a Flipkart manager gets a daily report of orders by city (periodic) and an alert when a warehouse runs out of a popular phone (exception).

**Conclusion:** whatever the type, an MIS gives the right manager the right summary at the right time.
