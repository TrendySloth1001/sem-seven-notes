**In simple words:** data governance means setting clear rules and owners for the company's data, so that it is correct, safe and used the same way everywhere. It is like traffic rules for a city: roads alone are not enough. <Cite id="data-management-ibm-data-governance" />

**Definition:** data governance is the set of policies, standards, roles and processes that decide how data is created, stored, used, protected and retired across the whole organization. <Cite id="data-management-wiki-data-governance" />

**How it is achieved in an MIS, step by step**

1. **Set goals:** for example "fewer billing errors" or "pass the privacy audit".
2. **Assign roles:** a steering committee on top, a data owner (a business manager) and a data steward (daily care) for each data area.
3. **Write policies and standards:** common definitions such as "what counts as an active customer", naming and format rules, access rules, how long to keep data.
4. **Build a data catalog:** one place that lists what data exists and what it means.
5. **Manage master data and quality:** keep one trusted "golden record" per customer or product and clean the data regularly. <Cite id="data-management-gfg-mdm" />
6. **Control access and security:** each role sees only the data it needs.
7. **Track lineage and audit:** record where data came from and check that rules are followed.

| Role | Job |
|---|---|
| Steering committee | Sets strategy and settles disputes |
| Data owner | Accountable for one data area |
| Data steward | Applies the rules day to day |

**Example:** a bank such as SBI keeps one customer record that every branch and app uses, and only authorised staff can open it.

**Conclusion:** governance gives the MIS one version of the truth, better decisions and lower risk of leaks.
