**In simple words:** wired technology sends data through physical cables; wireless technology sends it through the air using radio waves. Wired is steadier, wireless is more convenient. <Cite id="networks-wireless-gfg-wired-wireless" />

**Main differences**

| Point | Wired | Wireless |
|---|---|---|
| Medium | Physical cable (copper or fibre) | Air (radio, microwave, infrared, satellite) |
| Speed | High and steady | Usually lower, varies with distance and noise |
| Mobility | Fixed to a socket | Free to move within range |
| Installation | Slow and costly, cables must be laid | Fast and cheap, fit an access point |
| Reliability | High, little interference | Affected by walls, weather and other devices |
| Security | Better, needs physical access | Weaker, signal can be intercepted, so needs encryption |
| Cost to expand | More cable for each new point | Little extra cost for a new user |
| Standard | Ethernet (IEEE 802.3) | Wi-Fi (IEEE 802.11), Bluetooth, cellular |
| Example | Office desktop on a LAN socket | Phone on campus Wi-Fi |

<Cite id="networks-wireless-gfg-guided-unguided" /> <Cite id="networks-wireless-wiki-wireless-network" />

**In practice:** firms use both. Servers and core links are wired for speed and reliability; staff phones and laptops use Wi-Fi for convenience. <Cite id="networks-wireless-rainer-iis" />

**Conclusion:** choose wired when speed and security matter most, and wireless when mobility and quick setup matter most.
