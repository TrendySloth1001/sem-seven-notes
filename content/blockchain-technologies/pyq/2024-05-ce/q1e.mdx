**In simple words:** Bitcoin is **digital money** (a peer-to-peer cash system). Ethereum is a **programmable blockchain** (a world computer) that also has its own money, ether. <Cite id="bitcoin-gfg-bitcoin-vs-ethereum" />

| Point | Bitcoin | Ethereum |
|---|---|---|
| Creator and year | Satoshi Nakamoto, 2009 | Vitalik Buterin and others, 2015 |
| Main purpose | Digital cash, store of value | Platform for smart contracts and dApps |
| Coin | BTC | ETH |
| Consensus | Proof of Work | Proof of Stake (since the Merge, 2022) |
| Block time | About 10 minutes | About 12 seconds |
| Ledger model | UTXO (unspent outputs) | Account balance model |
| Scripting | Limited, not Turing-complete | Solidity on the EVM, Turing-complete (bounded by gas) |
| Supply | Capped at 21 million | No fixed cap; new issue is low and part of fees is burned |
| Fees | Per byte of transaction size | Per unit of gas |
| Language | Script | Solidity, Vyper |

<Cite id="bitcoin-educative-bitcoin-vs-ethereum" />

**Similarities:** both are public, permissionless, use cryptographic hashes and digital signatures, and keep a copy of the ledger on many nodes.

**Example:** use Bitcoin to send money across borders without a bank. Use Ethereum to run a lending app or issue an NFT, where a contract enforces the rules.

**Conclusion:** Bitcoin focuses on being secure, simple money; Ethereum focuses on being a flexible platform for any application. <Cite id="blockchain-fundamentals-nakamoto" /> <Cite id="ethereum-whitepaper" />
