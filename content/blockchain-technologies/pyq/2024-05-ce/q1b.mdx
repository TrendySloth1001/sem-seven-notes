**In simple words:** a wallet does not hold coins. It holds the **private keys** that let you spend coins recorded on the blockchain. The difference is whether those keys are kept on a device **connected to the internet** (hot) or **offline** (cold). <Cite id="wallets-utxo-double-spending-gfg-hot-cold" />

**Hot wallet:** keys live on a phone, laptop, browser extension or exchange server that is online. Examples: MetaMask, a mobile app, an exchange account. It is fast and easy for daily use, but a hacker, malware or a phishing site can reach the keys. <Cite id="wallets-utxo-double-spending-wiki-crypto-wallet" />

**Cold wallet:** keys are created and stored offline: a hardware wallet (Ledger, Trezor), a paper wallet, or an air-gapped computer. A transaction is signed offline and only the signed result is sent to the network. Online attackers cannot reach the keys. <Cite id="wallets-utxo-double-spending-btcwiki-cold" /> <Cite id="wallets-utxo-double-spending-wiki-hw-wallet" />

| Point | Hot wallet | Cold wallet |
|---|---|---|
| Internet | Always or often connected | Offline |
| Security | Lower: exposed to hacking and malware | Higher: protected from online attacks |
| Convenience | Very easy, instant payments | Slower: need to connect or sign offline |
| Best for | Small amounts, daily spending, trading | Large amounts, long-term saving |
| Cost | Mostly free | Hardware costs money |
| Main risk | Hack, phishing | Loss, theft or damage of the device, lost backup |

**Example:** keep pocket money in a hot wallet and your savings in a cold wallet, like cash in your pocket versus gold in a bank locker.

**Conclusion:** hot wallets favour convenience, cold wallets favour security. In both cases the **seed phrase** backup must be kept safe, because whoever has the keys owns the coins. <Cite id="wallets-utxo-double-spending-mb-ch05-wallets" />
