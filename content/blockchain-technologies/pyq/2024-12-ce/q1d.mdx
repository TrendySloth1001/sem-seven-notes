**In simple words:** a smart contract is a program stored on the blockchain that runs by itself when its conditions are met. Think of a vending machine: put in the money, press the button, and the snack drops, with no shopkeeper. The idea came from Nick Szabo (1994). <Cite id="smart-contracts-szabo-1994" />

**Key features:** automatic, no middleman, transparent, and cannot be changed once deployed (immutable). On Ethereum they are written in Solidity and run on the EVM. <Cite id="smart-contracts-eth-intro" />

**Types of smart contracts:**

1. **Smart legal contracts:** legal agreements turned into code, such as a rent or insurance agreement that pays out automatically.
2. **Decentralised autonomous organisations (DAOs):** contracts that hold funds and let members vote on how to use them.
3. **Application logic contracts (dApp contracts):** the back-end of a decentralised app, such as a token, a game or a lending pool.
4. **Deterministic contracts:** need no outside data; the same input always gives the same result (for example a token transfer).
5. **Non-deterministic contracts:** depend on outside data (price, weather) brought in by an oracle.
6. **Multi-signature contracts:** need approval from several owners before money moves.

Another common split is **on-chain** contracts (all code on the blockchain) and **off-chain** logic that only records results. <Cite id="smart-contracts-gfg-smart-contracts" />

**Conclusion:** smart contracts replace trust in people with trust in code. <Cite id="smart-contracts-ibm-smart-contracts" />
