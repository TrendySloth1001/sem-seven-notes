**In simple words:** a cryptocurrency is digital money that exists only on a blockchain. It is protected by cryptography, has no central bank, and ownership is proved by a private key. Bitcoin (2009) was the first. <Cite id="cryptocurrencies-tokens-wiki-crypto" />

**Features:** decentralised, secure (cryptography), transparent public ledger, borderless, pseudonymous, and often limited in supply.

**How it works (short):** you hold a wallet with keys; you sign a transaction; miners or validators check it and add it to a block; the new balance is recorded for everyone.

**Types of cryptocurrencies:**

1. **Coins (native coins):** run on their own blockchain and pay for its use. Examples: Bitcoin (BTC), Ether (ETH), Litecoin (LTC). Bitcoin is mostly a store of value; Litecoin is a faster payment coin. <Cite id="cryptocurrencies-tokens-gfg-coins-tokens" />
2. **Altcoins:** every coin other than Bitcoin, such as Litecoin, Ripple (XRP) and Cardano (ADA).
3. **Tokens:** built on another blockchain with a smart contract, not their own chain. ERC-20 tokens (fungible, for example USDT, LINK) and ERC-721 (non-fungible, NFTs). Utility tokens give access to a service; security tokens represent ownership such as shares. <Cite id="cryptocurrencies-tokens-eth-erc20" />
4. **Stablecoins:** value tied to a steady asset such as the US dollar (USDC, USDT, DAI), so the price does not swing much. <Cite id="cryptocurrencies-tokens-wiki-stablecoin" />
5. **Privacy coins:** hide sender, receiver and amount, such as Monero and Zcash.
6. **Meme coins:** started as jokes or social trends, such as Dogecoin; very risky.
7. **Central bank digital currencies (CBDC):** government-issued digital money; India's e-rupee is an example. It is not a decentralised cryptocurrency, but is related.

| Type | Own blockchain | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Coin | Yes | BTC, ETH |
| Token | No (uses another chain) | USDT, LINK |
| Stablecoin | Usually token | USDC, DAI |

**Conclusion:** cryptocurrencies range from money (coins) to application assets (tokens) to price-stable coins, each for a different need.
