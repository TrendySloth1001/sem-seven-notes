**In simple words:** a private blockchain is a blockchain that only invited, known members can join. Companies and banks need this because a fully public chain does not give them privacy, speed or control. <Cite id="private-blockchain-gfg-private" />

**Why a private blockchain is needed:**

1. **Privacy of business data.** On a public chain every transaction is visible to everyone. A company cannot show its prices, contracts or customers to rivals. A private chain shows data only to members.
2. **Known identities.** Rules (laws such as KYC and audit rules) need to know who is taking part. Members are identified and given permission.
3. **High speed and low cost.** Only a few trusted nodes agree, so they can use light consensus (such as Raft or PBFT) instead of mining. This gives high throughput and no mining fees.
4. **Control and governance.** The owner or consortium decides who can read, write and validate, and can change the rules or fix errors.
5. **Legal compliance.** Data rules (for example, data must stay in a country) can be followed because the nodes are known.
6. **Less energy.** No proof-of-work mining is needed.

| | Public | Private |
|---|---|---|
| Who can join | Anyone | Invited members |
| Speed | Slow | Fast |
| Privacy | Low | High |
| Consensus | PoW / PoS | PBFT, Raft |

<Cite id="private-blockchain-gfg-pubpriv" />

**Examples:** Hyperledger Fabric, Corda and Quorum are used for trade finance and supply chains. <Cite id="private-blockchain-fabric-whatis" />

**Limitation:** it is less decentralised, so members must trust the owners more.

**Conclusion:** private blockchains give the shared-ledger benefits while keeping privacy, speed and control that businesses require.
