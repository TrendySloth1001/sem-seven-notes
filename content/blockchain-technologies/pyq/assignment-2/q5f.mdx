**In simple words:** A start-up can sell digital tokens to the public to raise money. An ICO sells ordinary tokens; an STO sells tokens that stand for real ownership and follow the law. <Cite id="cryptocurrencies-tokens-gfg-ico" />

**ICO (Initial Coin Offering).** A way for a business to raise funds by selling newly created tokens, often for Bitcoin or Ether. <Cite id="class-bt-m5" loc="slide 31" /> Steps: prepare a whitepaper, create the token, decide supply and price, build a smart contract for the sale, market it, sell tokens, then use the funds. <Cite id="class-bt-m5" loc="slide 37" />

**STO (Security Token Offering).** Funds are raised by issuing tokens that represent securities or financial rights, following securities laws. <Cite id="class-bt-m5" loc="slide 44" /> It was introduced because ICOs saw fraud and weak regulation. <Cite id="class-bt-m5" loc="slide 44" />

| Point | ICO | STO |
|---|---|---|
| Token represents | Usually a utility, no ownership | Securities, ownership, profit rights |
| Regulation | Light or uncertain | Follows securities law |
| Investor checks | Often none | KYC and AML |
| Risk | High (scams, volatility) | Lower |
| Advantages | Easy fundraising, global investors, quick funds | Legal compliance, trust |

<Cite id="class-bt-m5" loc="slide 38" /> <Cite id="class-bt-m5" loc="slide 45" /> <Cite id="web-bt-2-wiki-security-token" />

**Role of tokens in fundraising:** they let small investors worldwide join, give quick access to funds, can be traded easily, and reduce the need for banks. Disadvantages are volatility, scams, and unclear rules. <Cite id="class-bt-m5" loc="slide 40" /> <Cite id="cryptocurrencies-tokens-gfg-security-token" />

**Conclusion:** ICOs are open and fast but risky; STOs add legal safety.
