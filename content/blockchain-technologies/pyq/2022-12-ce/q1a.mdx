**In simple words:** a blockchain is a shared notebook of records that many computers keep copies of. Nobody can quietly change an old page, because every page is locked to the one before it.

**Definition:** a blockchain is a distributed, append-only ledger. Transactions are grouped into blocks, and each block stores the hash of the previous block, so the blocks form a chain. The network agrees on the next block by a consensus rule, so no single party controls the record. <Cite id="blockchain-fundamentals-nakamoto" /> <Cite id="blockchain-fundamentals-ibm-blockchain" />

**Types of blockchain** differ in who may read, write and validate:

- **Public:** anyone can join, read and validate (Bitcoin, Ethereum).
- **Private:** one organisation owns it and decides who joins (an internal company ledger).
- **Consortium:** a group of known organisations share control (a bank group on Hyperledger Fabric).
- **Hybrid:** part is public, part is private, so some data stays hidden.

| Point | Public | Private | Consortium |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who can join | Anyone | Invited by one owner | Invited by the group |
| Control | Nobody (decentralised) | One organisation | Several organisations |
| Consensus | PoW, PoS (slow, costly) | Raft, PBFT (fast) | PBFT, Raft (fast) |
| Speed | Low | High | High |
| Privacy | Low, all is visible | High | Medium to high |
| Trust needed | None | Trust in the owner | Trust in the group |
| Example | Bitcoin | Internal audit ledger | Hyperledger Fabric network |

<Cite id="blockchain-fundamentals-gfg-types" /> <Cite id="private-blockchain-gfg-pubpriv" />

**Conclusion:** public chains give openness, private chains give speed and privacy, and consortium chains sit in between.
