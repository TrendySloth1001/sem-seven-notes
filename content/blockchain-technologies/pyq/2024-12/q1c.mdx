**In simple words:** mining difficulty tells how hard it is to find a valid block. The network adjusts it so that a new block always arrives at about the same speed, even when more or fewer miners join.

**Definition:** in proof of work, a miner changes a number (the nonce) until the hash of the block header is smaller than a **target**. A smaller target means a harder puzzle. Difficulty is how much smaller than the easiest possible target the current target is. <Cite id="bitcoin-btcwiki-difficulty" />

**Why it is needed:** if more miners join, blocks would come faster and faster. A fixed block time (about 10 minutes in Bitcoin) keeps the money supply and the confirmation time steady. <Cite id="blockchain-fundamentals-nakamoto" loc="§4" />

**How it is adjusted (Bitcoin):**

1. Every 2016 blocks, each node compares the actual time taken with the expected time (2016 x 10 minutes = 2 weeks).
2. New difficulty = old difficulty x (expected time / actual time). The change is limited to a factor of 4 up or down.
3. Faster than expected means higher difficulty; slower means lower. <Cite id="bitcoin-mb-ch12-mining" />

<Sum>

<Given>

The last 2016 blocks took 10 days. Expected time is 14 days. Old difficulty is $D$.

</Given>

<Find>

the new difficulty.

</Find>

<Steps>

1. Ratio $= \text{expected} / \text{actual} = 14 / 10 = 1.4$. *Reason: blocks came too fast, so we scale up.*
2. New difficulty $= D \times 1.4$. *Reason: the formula multiplies the old value by the ratio.*

</Steps>

<Answer>

new difficulty $= 1.4\,D$ (40% harder)

</Answer>

<Box kind="verify">

At $1.4\,D$ each block takes 1.4 times longer: $10 \times 1.4 = 14$ days for 2016 blocks, which is the 2-week goal.

</Box>

</Sum>

**Effect:** more hash power means higher difficulty and more energy use, and it makes attacks costly. <Cite id="consensus-mining-gfg-pow" />
