**In simple words:** in R, `c()` joins values into a vector, `data.frame()` builds a table from vectors, and `rbind()` adds more rows to a table. <Cite id="pyq-2025-05-ce-w3-r-vectors" /> <Cite id="pyq-2025-05-ce-gfg-r-df" />

**i. Enter the 10 salaries**

```r
emp <- data.frame(
  SrNo   = 1:10,
  Name   = c("Vivek", "Karan", "James", "Soham", "Renu",
             "Farah", "Hetal", "Mary", "Ganesh", "Krish"),
  Salary = c(21000, 55000, 67000, 50000, 54000,
             40000, 30000, 70000, 20000, 15000)
)
emp
```

Output:

```
   SrNo   Name Salary
1     1  Vivek  21000
2     2  Karan  55000
3     3  James  67000
4     4  Soham  50000
5     5   Renu  54000
6     6  Farah  40000
7     7  Hetal  30000
8     8   Mary  70000
9     9 Ganesh  20000
10   10  Krish  15000
```

(If only the salaries are needed, `sal <- c(21000, 55000, 67000, 50000, 54000, 40000, 30000, 70000, 20000, 15000)` is enough.)

**ii. Add 5 new employees.** The question gives no data, so we assume (any suitable data) the following new employees. `rbind()` stacks rows, and the column names must match.

```r
new <- data.frame(
  SrNo   = 11:15,
  Name   = c("Anita", "Rahul", "Sneha", "Imran", "Pooja"),
  Salary = c(45000, 38000, 62000, 27000, 33000)
)
emp <- rbind(emp, new)
emp
```

Output (the first 10 rows are unchanged, then):

```
   SrNo   Name Salary
11   11  Anita  45000
12   12  Rahul  38000
13   13  Sneha  62000
14   14  Imran  27000
15   15  Pooja  33000
```

`nrow(emp)` now gives `[1] 15`. <Cite id="pyq-2024-05-ce-r-cbind" />

**Note:** `rbind()` adds rows (same columns); `cbind()` adds columns; `merge()` joins two tables on a common column. For a plain vector, `c(sal, new_sal)` appends values.

**Conclusion:** `data.frame()` creates the table and `rbind()` joins the new records to it.
