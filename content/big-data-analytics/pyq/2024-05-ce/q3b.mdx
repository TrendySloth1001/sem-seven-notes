**In simple words:** data visualization shows data as charts, maps and dashboards so that people can see patterns, trends and odd values quickly instead of reading thousands of rows. <Cite id="pyq-2024-05-ce-ibm-dataviz" /> <Cite id="pyq-2024-05-ce-gfg-dataviz" />

**Why it is useful:** the human eye finds a rising line or a red spot faster than a number in a table. With big data, charts also summarise millions of records on one screen.

| Area | Application | Typical chart |
|---|---|---|
| Business | Sales by region and month, profit dashboards | Bar, line, dashboard |
| Healthcare | Spread of a disease, patient vitals, hospital load | Map, line chart |
| Finance | Stock prices, fraud alerts, risk | Candlestick, line |
| Marketing | Customer groups, campaign results, web traffic | Pie, funnel, heat map |
| Science and weather | Temperature trends, satellite data | Heat map, contour map |
| Government | Census, crime and election results | Choropleth map |
| Education | Marks distribution, pass rate by year | Histogram, bar |
| Data analysis and machine learning | Exploring data, finding outliers, comparing model accuracy | Scatter, box plot, histogram |
| Networks and social media | Who is connected to whom, trending topics | Graph, word cloud |

**Benefits:** quick understanding, easy comparison, spotting outliers, simple communication to non-technical people, and real-time monitoring of live data.

**Limitations:** a wrong chart type or a cut axis can mislead, and very big data may need sampling or aggregation first.

**Conclusion:** visualization turns large raw data into pictures that support faster and better decisions in business, health, finance, science and government. <Cite id="pyq-2024-05-ce-wiki-dataviz" />
