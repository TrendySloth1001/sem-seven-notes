**In simple words:** DGIM counts the 1s in the last $N$ bits of a stream without storing the bits. It keeps a few **buckets**, each written as (size, timestamp of its right end), where the size is the number of 1s it covers. A bucket list is valid only if it obeys six constraints. <Cite id="stream-counting-mmds-home" loc="§4.6" /> <Cite id="stream-counting-cs246-slides" />

**The six constraints**

1. **The right end of a bucket is always a 1.** A bucket never ends in a 0.
2. **Every 1 in the window belongs to some bucket.** No 1 is left uncounted.
3. **No position belongs to more than one bucket.** Buckets do not overlap.
4. **There are one or two buckets of each size**, up to some maximum size. Three of one size must be merged.
5. **Every bucket size is a power of 2:** 1, 2, 4, 8 and so on.
6. **Sizes never decrease going back in time.** From newest to oldest the sizes stay equal or grow.

(A bucket is also dropped when its right end leaves the window of the last $N$ bits.) <Cite id="stream-counting-wiki-dsm" />

**Example.** Stream (oldest first), times 1 to 8: `1 0 1 1 0 1 1 1`. The 1s are at times 1, 3, 4, 6, 7 and 8. Buckets, newest first, as (size, right end): (1, 8), (1, 7), (2, 6), (2, 3).

| Constraint | Check |
|---|---|
| 1 | Right ends 8, 7, 6 and 3 are all 1s. |
| 2 and 3 | The buckets cover the 1s at 8; 7; 6 and 4; 3 and 1. That is all six 1s, each once. |
| 4 | Two buckets of size 1 and two of size 2, never three of a size. |
| 5 | Sizes 1, 1, 2, 2 are powers of 2. |
| 6 | Newest to oldest, 1, 1, 2, 2 never decrease. |

The sizes add up to $1 + 1 + 2 + 2 = 6$, the number of 1s. An invalid list is 1, 2, 2, 2 (three buckets of size 2) or 1, 3 (3 is not a power of 2). <Cite id="stream-counting-wiki-streaming" />

**Conclusion:** these six constraints keep the number of buckets near $\log N$ while still allowing a window count within 50 percent error.
