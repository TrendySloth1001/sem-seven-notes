**In simple words:** CURE (Clustering Using REpresentatives, Guha, Rastogi and Shim, 1998) is a clustering algorithm for large data. It represents each cluster by a fixed number $c$ of **well-scattered points**, each moved a fraction $\alpha$ of the way towards the cluster centre. Because it uses several points and not one centre, it finds clusters of odd shapes and sizes, and shrinking makes it robust to outliers. <Cite id="cure-stream-clustering-guha1998" loc="§1.2" /> <Cite id="cure-stream-clustering-tp-cure" />

**Why it is needed:** k-means and centroid methods find round, equal-sized clusters and split long ones; the all-points method chains through bridges and is hurt by outliers. CURE sits between these two extremes.

<Img id="img-bda-n3-cure-nonround" caption="On two curved clusters a centroid method mixes the colours in the middle, which is the failure CURE avoids." />

**Algorithm**

1. **Sample.** Take a random sample that fits in memory.
2. **Cluster the sample** hierarchically: start with each point as its own cluster and merge the closest pair until $k$ clusters remain.
3. **Pick $c$ well-scattered points** per cluster: first the point farthest from the centroid, then each next point farthest from those already chosen.
4. **Shrink** each point towards the centroid: $p_{\text{new}} = p + \alpha(\text{centroid} - p)$. Far points (outliers) move the most.
5. **Merge** clusters whose closest pair of representatives is closest, choose new representatives, and repeat until $k$ clusters remain.
6. **Label** every other point with the cluster of its nearest representative, in one scan of the data.

<Sum>

<Given>

One cluster of six points: $A(1,2)$, $B(3,2)$, $C(5,2)$, $D(9,2)$, $E(4,6)$, $F(2,-2)$. Take $c = 3$ representatives and $\alpha = 0.2$.

</Given>

<Find>

the centroid, the three scattered points and the representatives.

</Find>

<Steps>

1. **Centroid.** $x$: $(1+3+5+9+4+2)/6 = 4$. $y$: $(2+2+2+2+6-2)/6 = 2$. Centroid $(4,2)$.
2. **First point: farthest from the centroid.** Squared distances: A 9, B 1, C 1, D 25, E 16, F 20. The largest is $D(9,2)$.
3. **Second: farthest from D.** Squared distances to D: A 64, B 36, C 16, E 41, F 65. The largest is $F(2,-2)$.
4. **Third: farthest from the nearest chosen point.** Smaller of the squared distances to D and F: A 17, B 17, C 16, E 41. The largest is $E(4,6)$.
5. **Shrink 20 percent** with $p + 0.2(\text{centroid} - p)$: D $\to (9 + 0.2 \times (-5), 2) = (8,2)$; F $\to (2 + 0.2 \times 2, -2 + 0.2 \times 4) = (2.4,-1.2)$; E $\to (4, 6 + 0.2 \times (-4)) = (4,5.2)$.

</Steps>

<Answer>

Centroid $(4,2)$; scattered points $D(9,2)$, $F(2,-2)$, $E(4,6)$; representatives $(8,2)$, $(2.4,-1.2)$, $(4,5.2)$.

</Answer>

<Box kind="verify">

Each representative lies on the line from its point to the centroid; for E the line is $x = 4$ and the representative has $x = 4$. The far point D moved 1 unit (20 percent of 5), while a nearer point would move less. The x total in another order: $(1+9)+(3+5)+(4+2) = 24$, and $24/6 = 4$.

</Box>

</Sum>

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Finds clusters of any shape and size | Needs a Euclidean space (it uses centroids) |
| Less sensitive to outliers | Depends on $c$, $\alpha$, sample size and $k$ |
| Scales by sampling and partitioning | A small cluster can be missed by the sample |

<Cite id="cure-stream-clustering-wiki-cure" />

**Conclusion:** CURE clusters a sample using shrunken representative points and then labels all data by the nearest representative, giving good clusters on big data.
