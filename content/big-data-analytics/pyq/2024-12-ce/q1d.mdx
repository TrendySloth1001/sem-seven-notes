**In simple words:** **DGIM** (Datar, Gionis, Indyk, Motwani) counts the 1s in the last $N$ bits of a 0/1 stream without storing the bits. It keeps only a few **buckets**. Each bucket stores its **size** (the number of 1s it covers) and the **timestamp of its right (newest) end**. A bucket list is valid only if it follows these rules. <Cite id="stream-counting-mmds-home" loc="§4.6" /> <Cite id="stream-counting-wiki-dsm" />

1. **The right end of every bucket is a 1.** A bucket never ends in a 0.
2. **No position is in more than one bucket.** Buckets do not overlap.
3. **There are one or two buckets of each size**, up to some maximum size. Three of one size must be merged.
4. **Every size is a power of 2** (1, 2, 4, 8, ...). A bucket of size 3 is not allowed.
5. **Sizes never decrease going back in time.** From newest to oldest the sizes stay the same or grow.

(The usual full list also says that every 1 in the window belongs to some bucket, and a bucket is dropped once its right end leaves the window.) <Cite id="stream-counting-cs246-slides" />

**Example.** A valid list, newest first, as (size, right-end timestamp): (1, 50), (1, 48), (2, 45), (4, 38), (4, 30). Sizes 1, 1, 2, 4, 4 are powers of 2, at most two of each, and never decrease.

| Invalid list (sizes, newest first) | Rule broken |
|---|---|
| 1, 2, 2, 2, 4 | Three buckets of size 2 (rule 3) |
| 1, 3, 4 | 3 is not a power of 2 (rule 4) |
| 2, 1, 1 | An older bucket is smaller (rule 5) |

**Conclusion:** these rules keep the memory small (about $O(\log^2 N)$ bits) and the count within 50 percent of the true value.
