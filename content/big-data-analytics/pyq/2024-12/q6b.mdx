**In simple words:** collaborative filtering recommends a movie to you because people with tastes like yours liked it. It does not need to know anything about the movie itself, only who rated what. <Cite id="recommendation-systems-wiki-cf" /> <Cite id="recommendation-systems-google-cf" />

**Steps (user-based)**

1. Build the **utility matrix**: rows are users, columns are movies, entries are ratings; most are empty.
2. Compute the **similarity** between the target user and every other user, using the movies both rated (cosine similarity, or Pearson correlation after subtracting each user's average rating).
3. Pick the most similar users (neighbours).
4. **Predict** the missing rating as the similarity-weighted average of the neighbours' ratings of that movie.
5. Recommend the movies with the highest predicted ratings. <Cite id="recommendation-systems-av" />

<Sum>

<Given>

Ratings (1 to 5); "?" means not rated.

| | M1 | M2 | M3 | M4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| U1 | 5 | 3 | ? | 4 |
| U2 | 4 | 2 | 5 | 4 |
| U3 | 1 | 5 | 2 | 1 |

</Given>

<Find>

U1's predicted rating for M3, using cosine similarity on the movies both users rated.

</Find>

<Steps>

1. **Common movies** for U1 with U2 and with U3 are M1, M2, M4. U1 = (5, 3, 4), U2 = (4, 2, 4), U3 = (1, 5, 1).
2. **cos(U1, U2).** Dot product $= 20 + 6 + 16 = 42$. Lengths: $\sqrt{25+9+16} = \sqrt{50} = 7.071$ and $\sqrt{16+4+16} = 6$. So $42 / (7.071 \times 6) = 0.990$.
3. **cos(U1, U3).** Dot product $= 5 + 15 + 4 = 24$. Length of U3 $= \sqrt{1+25+1} = \sqrt{27} = 5.196$. So $24 / (7.071 \times 5.196) = 0.653$.
4. **Weighted average** of their M3 ratings (U2 gave 5, U3 gave 2): $(0.990 \times 5 + 0.653 \times 2) / (0.990 + 0.653) = 6.256 / 1.643 = 3.81$.

</Steps>

<Answer>

predicted rating of M3 for U1 $\approx 3.81$, so M3 is a reasonable recommendation (above the middle rating of 3).

</Answer>

<Box kind="verify">

The weights are 0.602 for U2 and 0.398 for U3, so $0.602 \times 5 + 0.398 \times 2 = 3.01 + 0.80 = 3.81$, the same value. It lies between 2 and 5 and nearer to 5, since U2 is more similar to U1.

</Box>

</Sum>

**Types and limits.** Item-based filtering compares movies instead of users and is more stable for large systems. Collaborative filtering suffers from the **cold start** problem (new users or movies have no ratings), **sparsity** (few ratings) and scale. Raw-rating cosine ignores that some users rate high and others low; Pearson fixes this. <Cite id="recommendation-systems-wiki-pearson" />

**Conclusion:** collaborative filtering predicts a user's rating from similar users' ratings, as in Netflix-style movie recommendation.
