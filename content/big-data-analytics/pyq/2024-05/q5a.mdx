**In simple words:** collaborative filtering (CF) recommends items using **only the ratings of many users**, not any facts about the items. The idea is that people who agreed in the past will agree in the future. User $x$ gets what users **similar to $x$** liked. <Cite id="recommendation-systems-wiki-cf" /> <Cite id="recommendation-systems-google-cf" /> <Cite id="recommendation-systems-mmds-slides" />

**Analogy.** You and your friend Riya loved the same four films. She loved a fifth, so you trust her and watch it.

**Data used:** the **utility matrix**, a table of users by items holding ratings, mostly empty.

**Steps (user-user CF)**

1. Find the users whose ratings are most similar to user $x$ (set $N$), using cosine or Pearson (centered cosine) similarity.
2. Predict $x$'s rating of an unseen item as the similarity-weighted average of the ratings of $N$: $r_{xi} = \dfrac{\sum_{y \in N} s_{xy} r_{yi}}{\sum_{y \in N} s_{xy}}$.
3. Recommend the items with the highest predictions.

**Item-item CF** does the same with similar items: find items like the ones $x$ rated and average $x$'s own ratings of them.

<Sum>

<Given>

Ratings (blank = not rated). Predict Alice's rating of M4 using centered-cosine similarity.

| User | M1 | M2 | M3 | M4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Alice | 5 | 3 | 4 | ? |
| Bob | 5 | 2 | 4 | 5 |
| Carol | 1 | 5 | 3 | 3 |
| Dan | 4 | 3 | 5 | 4 |

</Given>

<Find>

Alice's predicted rating of M4.

</Find>

<Steps>

1. Row averages: Alice 4, Bob 4, Carol 3, Dan 4. Subtract them (centering); Alice's blank stays 0. Alice $(1, -1, 0, 0)$, Bob $(1, -2, 0, 1)$, Carol $(-2, 2, 0, 0)$, Dan $(0, -1, 1, 0)$.
2. Similarity with Alice: Bob $= 3/(\sqrt{2}\sqrt{6}) \approx 0.866$; Carol $= -4/(\sqrt{2}\sqrt{8}) = -1$; Dan $= 1/(\sqrt{2}\sqrt{2}) = 0.5$.
3. Use the two most similar users, Bob and Dan (Carol has opposite taste).
4. Prediction $= \dfrac{0.866 \times 5 + 0.5 \times 4}{0.866 + 0.5} = \dfrac{6.33}{1.366} \approx 4.63$.

</Steps>

<Answer>

predicted rating of M4 for Alice $\approx 4.63$, so M4 is a strong recommendation.

</Answer>

<Box kind="verify">

The answer must lie between the neighbours' ratings 4 and 5, and nearer Bob's 5 because Bob is more similar: 4.63 does. The unweighted average $(5 + 4)/2 = 4.5$ is a little lower, as expected.

</Box>

</Sum>

**How it differs from content-based filtering**

| Point | Content-based | Collaborative filtering |
|---|---|---|
| Uses | Item **properties** and the user's own history | **Ratings of many users** only |
| Idea | Items similar to what you liked | Users or items with similar rating patterns |
| Needs item features | Yes | No |
| Needs other users | No | Yes, many |
| New item | Can be recommended at once if its features are known | Cannot, until someone rates it |
| New user | Cannot (no profile) | Cannot (no history) |
| Variety | Low, over-specialises | Higher, can surprise |
| Example | Pandora matching song attributes | Amazon "customers who bought this also bought" |

**Limits of CF:** the cold-start problem and a sparse matrix. Real systems often combine both methods (hybrid).

**Conclusion:** collaborative filtering recommends from the crowd's ratings, content-based from item features; each fixes the other's weakness.
