**In simple words:** CURE (Clustering Using REpresentatives, Guha, Rastogi aur Shim, 1998) Euclidean space mein bade data ke liye clustering algorithm hai. Har cluster ke liye ek centre (k-means) ya saare points (single link) rakhne ki jagah ye kuch **well-scattered points** rakhta hai aur unhe **centroid ki taraf shrink** kar deta hai. Ye **representative points** tay karte hain ki kaunse clusters merge honge. <Cite id="cure-stream-clustering-guha1998" loc="§1.2" /> <Cite id="cure-stream-clustering-wiki-cure" />

**Ye kyun zaroori hai:** k-means round, barabar size ke clusters dhoondta hai aur lambe ya bade clusters ko tod deta hai. Single link bridges ke through chain bana leta hai aur outliers se kharab hota hai. CURE kisi bhi shape aur size ke clusters dhoondta hai aur outliers ko jhelta hai. <Cite id="cure-stream-clustering-gfg-cure" />

<Img id="img-bda-n3-cure-nonround" caption="Do curved clusters par k-means jaisa centroid method beech ke points mila deta hai; CURE aise shapes ko follow karne ke liye bana hai." />

**Steps**

1. **Sample.** Ek random sample lo jo main memory mein aa sake.
2. **Sample ko cluster karo** hierarchically: har point ek cluster se shuru karo aur sabse paas wali pair ko merge karte jao jab tak $k$ clusters na bachein.
3. **Har cluster mein $c$ scattered points chuno:** pehla centroid se sabse door wala point, agla un points se sabse door wala jo pehle chune ja chuke hain.
4. Har ek ko centroid ki taraf $\alpha$ fraction **shrink** karo: $p_{new} = p + \alpha(\text{centroid} - p)$. Door wale points (outliers) sabse zyada hilte hain.
5. Jin clusters ke closest representatives sabse paas hon unhe **merge** karo; merged cluster ke naye representatives chuno; $k$ clusters bachne tak dohrao.
6. Poore data ke baaki har point ko uske nearest representative wale cluster ka **label** do.

<Sum>

<Given>

Ek cluster: $A(1,2)$, $B(3,2)$, $C(5,2)$, $D(9,2)$, $E(4,6)$, $F(2,-2)$. $c = 3$ representatives aur $\alpha = 0.2$ lo.

</Given>

<Find>

centroid, 3 scattered points aur 3 representative points.

</Find>

<Steps>

1. **Centroid.** $x$: $(1+3+5+9+4+2)/6 = 4$; $y$: $(2+2+2+2+6-2)/6 = 2$. Centroid $(4, 2)$.
2. **Pehla point: centroid se sabse door.** Squared distances: $A$ 9, $B$ 1, $C$ 1, $D$ 25, $E$ 16, $F$ 20. Sabse bada: $D(9,2)$.
3. **Doosra: $D$ se sabse door.** $D$ se squared distances: $A$ 64, $B$ 36, $C$ 16, $E$ 41, $F$ 65. Sabse bada: $F(2,-2)$.
4. **Teesra: apne nearest chune hue point se sabse door.** $A$ $\min(64, 17) = 17$; $B$ $\min(36, 17) = 17$; $C$ $\min(16, 25) = 16$; $E$ $\min(41, 68) = 41$. Sabse bada: $E(4,6)$.
5. **20 percent shrink** karo $p + 0.2(\text{centroid} - p)$ se: $D \to (9 - 1, 2) = (8, 2)$; $F \to (2 + 0.4, -2 + 0.8) = (2.4, -1.2)$; $E \to (4, 6 - 0.8) = (4, 5.2)$.

</Steps>

<Answer>

centroid $(4, 2)$; scattered points $D(9,2)$, $F(2,-2)$, $E(4,6)$; representatives $(8, 2)$, $(2.4, -1.2)$, $(4, 5.2)$.

</Answer>

<Box kind="verify">

Sabse door point $D$ 1 unit hila (uski doori 5 ka 20 percent), aur $F$ $0.2 \times \sqrt{20} \approx 0.89$ hila, to door points zyada hile. Har representative apne point aur centroid ko jodne wali line pe hai: $E$ aur centroid dono ka $x = 4$ hai, aur $(4, 5.2)$ ka bhi. Centroid khud, $24/6 = 4$ aur $12/6 = 2$, shrinking se nahi badalta.

</Box>

</Sum>

**$\alpha$ ka role:** $\alpha = 0$ points ko jagah pe rakhta hai (all-points method, outlier-sensitive); $\alpha = 1$ sab centroid pe le aata hai (centroid method). Paper mein 0.2 se 0.7 accha mila. <Cite id="cure-stream-clustering-guha1998" loc="§1.2" />

**Advantages:** koi bhi shape aur size, outliers ke liye robust, sampling se scale hota hai. **Disadvantages:** sirf Euclidean space, $c$, $\alpha$ aur sample size pe depend karta hai, chhota cluster chhoot sakta hai. <Cite id="cure-stream-clustering-tp-cure" />

**Conclusion:** CURE har cluster ko kuch shrunken scattered points se represent karta hai, jisse ajeeb shapes ke accha clusters milte hain aur sampling se bada data handle hota hai.

Exam mein English mein likho: "CURE represents each cluster by a fixed number of well-scattered points shrunk towards the centroid, merges clusters with the closest representatives, and uses sampling for large data."
