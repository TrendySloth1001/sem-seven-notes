**In simple words:** CURE (Clustering Using REpresentatives, Guha, Rastogi and Shim, 1998) is a hierarchical clustering method for large data. It describes each cluster by a few well-scattered points, moved a little towards the centre, and uses them to decide merges. <Cite id="cure-stream-clustering-guha1998" loc="§1.2" /> <Cite id="cure-stream-clustering-wiki-cure" />

**Why it is needed.** K-means and centroid methods like round clusters of equal size and split long or big ones. Using every point lets one stray point or thin bridge join clusters wrongly. CURE sits between the two: a handful of representatives capture the shape and are fast. <Cite id="cure-stream-clustering-guha1998" loc="§1.1" />

**Steps** (Euclidean space). <Cite id="distance-measures-mmds" loc="ch. 7" />

1. Take a random sample that fits in memory.
2. Cluster the sample hierarchically (merge the closest pair again and again) until $k$ clusters are left.
3. In each cluster pick $c$ well-scattered points: the first is farthest from the centroid, each next is farthest from those already chosen.
4. Shrink each point a fraction $\alpha$ towards the centroid, $p_{\text{new}} = p + \alpha(\text{centroid} - p)$. Far outliers move most.
5. The distance of two clusters is the distance of their closest pair of representatives. Merge the closest two and choose new representatives.
6. Scan all data once and put each point in the cluster of its nearest representative.

<Cite id="cure-stream-clustering-gfg-cure" loc="diagram" />

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Finds long or curved clusters, not only round ones | Needs a Euclidean space (it uses centroids) |
| Handles clusters of very different sizes | Depends on $c$, $\alpha$, sample size and $k$ |
| Less sensitive to outliers | A small cluster may be missed by a small sample |
| Scales by sampling and partitioning | Many distance computations in high dimensions |

<Cite id="cure-stream-clustering-tp-cure" />

**Conclusion:** CURE often finds better shaped clusters than k-means or BIRCH, at the price of tuning a few parameters.
