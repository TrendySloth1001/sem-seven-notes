**In simple words:** DGIM counts the 1s in the last N bits of a stream without storing the bits. It stores a few **buckets**, and the buckets must follow six rules. <Cite id="stream-counting-mmds-home" loc="§4.6" /> <Cite id="stream-counting-cs246-slides" />

A bucket is a pair (size, timestamp of its right end). Its size is the number of 1s it covers.

**The six constraints.**

1. **The right end of a bucket is always a 1.** A bucket never ends in a 0.
2. **Every 1 is in some bucket.** No 1 is left uncounted.
3. **No position is in more than one bucket.** Buckets do not overlap.
4. **There are one or two buckets of each size**, up to some maximum size. Three of one size are merged.
5. **Every size is a power of 2:** 1, 2, 4, 8 and so on.
6. **Sizes never decrease going back in time.** From newest to oldest the sizes stay equal or grow.

**Example.** Stream (oldest on the left): `1 0 1 1 0 1 1 1`, timestamps 1 to 8. It has six 1s, at positions 1, 3, 4, 6, 7 and 8. Buckets, newest first, as (size, right end): (1, 8), (1, 7), (2, 6), (2, 3).

| Rule | Check |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | The right ends 8, 7, 6 and 3 are all bits equal to 1. |
| 2 and 3 | The buckets cover the 1s at 8; 7; 6 and 4; 3 and 1. That is all six 1s, each once. |
| 4 | Two buckets of size 1 and two of size 2, so never more than two of a size. |
| 5 | The sizes 1, 1, 2, 2 are powers of 2. |
| 6 | Going back in time the sizes 1, 1, 2, 2 never decrease. |

The total of the sizes is 1 + 1 + 2 + 2 = 6, equal to the number of 1s.

**Conclusion:** these six rules keep the number of buckets small (about log N) while still allowing a count within 50 percent error. <Cite id="stream-counting-wiki-dsm" />
